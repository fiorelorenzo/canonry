Why this is a decision

A first import of the Valdoria Reach vault produces around 200 proposals in one run: new entities, new relations, none of them matched against anything because there is nothing to match against yet. Guardrail 1 still applies at full strength: explicit accept, per entry, no accept-all default. A GM staring down 200 individual clicks will not do it in one evening, and the product's own competitor is a tool that asked for zero clicks. This page has to make review fast without ever becoming the thing guardrail 1 forbids.

Guardrail 1 draws a sharp, useful line once you look for it: it forbids applying an unread change, not discarding one. Rejecting a proposal writes nothing to canon, so a bulk reject carries none of the risk a bulk accept does. Every option below is built on that asymmetry: grouping and filtering are navigation, rejection can batch, acceptance never does. C6 already fixed the actual accept mechanism (c6-accept-and-reject.html, the focused queue with j/k/a/r and an undo toast) for both propagation and import. This page does not get to invent a second way to accept a proposal, only a better way to reach the 200th one.

Options

A

Grouped by type

The 200 sit in five collapsible groups by entity type. Each group's header has a "Review" button that opens the C6 queue scoped to that group. A group can also be bulk-rejected outright; it can never be bulk-accepted.

Buys

  • The shape of the batch is visible at a glance: five numbers, five progress states, before reading a single proposal.
  • Bulk reject on a group the GM has skimmed and distrusts (the truncated relations) clears real noise in one action, honestly, without touching canon.
  • "38 of 38 reviewed" is a natural resting point, a GM can do one group and stop without losing their place.

Costs

  • 200 proposals rarely sort cleanly into five buckets; a character-shaped entity mentioned only in a relation still has to live somewhere.
  • Five separate queues (one per group) means five separate progress counters to reconcile into "40 of 200".
  • The temptation to add "Accept all" next to "Reject all" is one PR away at every group header; the asymmetry has to be enforced, not just designed.
B

One queue, C6 vocabulary, filtered

The exact focused queue from C6, unchanged, with type filter chips above it instead of grouping. j/k moves within whatever the chip selects; the pill list shows a window around the current position rather than all 200.

Buys

  • Zero new interaction to learn for a GM who already reviewed a propagation plan: same keys, same card, same undo.
  • The chips are pure filters on one continuous position, so switching from "Relations" back to "All" never loses "40 accepted so far".
  • Scales past 200 without a redesign; a thousand-document world just makes the counter bigger.

Costs

  • No resting point shaped like "a group is done"; 200 one-at-a-time decisions can still feel like a single long tunnel.
  • The windowed pill list loses A's at-a-glance sense of the whole batch's shape.
  • Nothing here handles "these 20 are obviously garbage, get rid of them together" any faster than 20 individual `r` presses.
C

Table, multi-select

Every proposal is a row: type, name, evidence. Checkboxes select rows. The bulk toolbar offers exactly one destructive action, reject, and one navigation action, open the queue on the selection. There is no accept checkbox path.

Buys

  • Best surface for triage: sort or scan by type and evidence quality before deciding how to spend the review session.
  • Selecting the two truncated relations and rejecting them together is exactly the "clear the noise fast" case A's flagged group also solves, with finer control.
  • "Open queue on selection" reuses C6 rather than adding a second accept path, satisfying the same constraint as A and B.

Costs

  • A checkbox column next to rows with visible Accept buttons is one design review away from someone proposing "Accept selected" for symmetry; it has to be argued down every time, not just today.
  • Dense rows read worse than A's badges or B's focused card for the evidence sentence, the thing guardrail 3 actually asks the GM to weigh.
  • Two hundred table rows is real virtual-scroll engineering for what is, underneath, the same one queue.

Rejected outright

"Accept selected" next to "Reject selected" in the table's bulk toolbar

It is the single most obvious feature request this screen will get, because it looks exactly as safe as the reject button sitting right next to it. It is not: rejecting a proposal writes nothing, accepting one writes prose or a relation to canon that nobody read. Guardrail 1 forbids it outright, and C6 already rejected the same shape at ten entries ("An 'Accept all 7' button behind a confirmation dialog"). At 200 entries the blast radius is twenty times larger and the temptation to add the button is proportionally stronger, which is exactly why it has to be named and refused here rather than left to be quietly reinvented.

What I would take

B, C6's queue with filter chips, A's grouping folded in as the chips

I would take B. C6 already decided the accept mechanism and decided it for both propagation and import; re-deriving a different one for D4 would make the product inconsistent with itself for no real gain. B's filter chips give most of A's at-a-glance shape (five counts, visible at the top) without forking into five separate queues to reconcile. I would keep A's per-group "Reject all" as a capability inside B: a chip's context menu offers "Reject all N shown" whenever a filter is active, which gets A's noise-clearing benefit without a second screen. C's table earns a place too, but as a triage view reachable before the queue, not as the primary review surface: its real job is finding the twenty truncated relations fast, then handing them to the same queue or the same bulk reject.

Cheapest way to find out this is wrong: ship B alone first, since it is the smallest addition to a component that already exists. Watch whether GMs stall out mid-batch without a table to triage from. If most 200-proposal sessions end with "reviewed 60, gave up," the table's sorting and bulk reject are worth building next; if GMs finish the queue in one sitting, they were never blocked on triage and C is not worth its cost.

What this locks in

  • #42 ships as "bulk" meaning bulk reject and bulk navigation only; the issue's own title says "bulk accept and reject review screen", and this page is the record that accept never actually batches, only reject does.
  • Hard to undo: once C6's queue is the fixed vocabulary for both surfaces, giving D4 a different accept interaction later (say, a swipe gesture unique to import) breaks the muscle memory C6 explicitly protects.
  • The type-grouping in the fixture (characters, places, factions, events, relations) becomes a real taxonomy the extraction pipeline has to tag consistently, or the filter chips in B have nothing reliable to filter on.
  • Deferred on purpose: what happens when 4 of the 200 are actually conflicts against existing entries rather than clean new ones, covered in D5; what happens when a proposed entity might already exist under a different name, covered in D6.
