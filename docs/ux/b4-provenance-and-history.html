Why this is a decision

SPEC 4.2 and 4.4 hand this page two tables that live at different depths of the product. fact is the atomic layer the Loremaster reasons over: a statement plus the exact span of the sentence it came from. revision is the entry's own timeline, and from 4.4 and Guardrail 2 it must say whether each entry was written by a human or came from an accepted AI proposal. Neither is optional to build. What is open is how much of the fact layer a GM ever has to look at directly, and what "tracked" means once the click that accepted a change is a month in the past.

This is not C5's job repeated. C5 already guarantees every open proposal shows its evidence span while the GM is deciding whether to accept it. This page is about what happens to that same idea once nothing is pending any more: is the fact layer a place a GM can go back to, and does the entry body remember, visually, that a sentence in it was once someone else's draft.

Guardrail 2 says AI text is visually distinct until accepted, and stays tracked afterwards. The pre-accept marking is C1's violet, and this page does not redesign it. But "tracked" cannot mean "the violet never comes off." An entry that reads three-quarters purple a year in is not a good wiki with the AI on, and Guardrail 4 says it has to still be one with the AI off. Tracked has to survive as a record you can go find, not a colour you have to keep looking at.

Options

A

Nowhere

The extracted fact layer has no GM-facing surface at all. It exists for retrieval and propagation; a GM who wants provenance opens History. The entry body carries no marker of any kind once a change is accepted.

Buys

  • The entry is the entry. Nothing about reading it ever hints at machinery underneath.
  • Cheapest to build and impossible to get visually noisy.
  • Closest to "AI switched off, still a good wiki" of the three by construction.

Costs

  • A GM who half-remembers "the copilot wrote some of this" has one path, and it is a click away from the text, not next to it.
  • Facts extracted for propagation are invisible even when a GM would genuinely want to check one, e.g. before trusting a Loremaster answer.
  • Feels like the marking was simply removed, not preserved, unless History is genuinely easy to find.
B

On demand

A closed-by-default "Facts" toggle in the aside, and a small neutral dot on a paragraph whose last revision was ai_accepted. Neither is loud; both are one click from nothing to full detail.

Buys

  • The evidence span sits right in the paragraph it belongs to, one hover away.
  • Facts are real and reachable for the GM who wants to audit the extraction, without being furniture for the GM who doesn't.
  • Scales: whether an entry has 3 facts or 40, the aside cost is the same one toggle.

Costs

  • A second, quieter marking system next to C1's violet asks a GM to learn what a grey dot means as well.
  • Easy to forget it exists; "on demand" only helps if a GM remembers to demand it.
  • The dot still needs a defensible rule for which paragraph it sits on when one revision touches several.
C

Always, in a margin

A permanent "Facts" panel in the aside, the same rail B3 uses for Relations. Hovering a fact highlights its span in the body; the panel doubles as a compact provenance list, so both related surfaces share one piece of real estate.

Buys

  • Nothing to remember to open; provenance and evidence are just there.
  • A GM auditing a contested entry (after an audit flag, say) never has to hunt for the extraction behind it.
  • One rail, two related jobs, instead of two separate affordances to design and explain.

Costs

  • Directly competes with B3's Relations panel for the same aside on the same page. Both cannot be "always on" without the rail growing three or four sections tall on every entry.
  • Makes the extracted layer part of the furniture of every entry, whether or not anyone asked for it, which is exactly the density guardrail 4 wants to avoid.
  • Facts nobody ever clicks still cost vertical space on every single page.

Rejected outright

The violet stays on the paragraph forever

The simplest reading of "tracked" is "keep marking it", so the AI paragraph just never loses C1's tint. It breaks Guardrail 4 on a scale no single decision should: a world worked on for a year would read mostly purple, which is not a wiki anyone keeps, AI on or off. It also quietly breaks the meaning of the colour itself: violet stops meaning "review this" and starts meaning "the copilot touched this once", which makes the thing guardrail 2 actually cares about, the pending review, harder to spot in the noise.

A "Facts" browser as a primary section, next to Entries and Works

Tempting because the extracted layer is genuinely the thing that makes propagation possible, and engineers like a window onto structure they built. But a table of atomic statements extracted by a model is not what a GM opens the product to read, and promoting it to primary navigation makes the machinery the product's face instead of the canon it maintains. With the AI switched off (Guardrail 4) that whole section is dead weight with nothing generating new rows into it.

What I would take

B, on-demand facts, with C's non-violet history badge on every option

I would take B. The history badge language: grey for human, calm green for accepted AI, never violet. Is not really optional and I would ship it regardless of which facts option wins; it is the actual answer to "what does tracked look like a month later." Facts specifically I would keep on demand rather than always-on, mainly because of the aside contention with B3: if B3 takes the margin for Relations (my recommendation there), the same rail cannot also carry a permanent Facts panel without the entry page reading like a debugging console. On demand keeps the capability real without making every entry pay for it whether or not the GM asked.

Cheapest way to find out this is wrong: watch whether GMs who reject a propagation diff (C7) ever go looking for the fact behind it afterwards. If "on demand" means they never find it and trust in the Loremaster's evidence erodes instead, that is the signal to promote Facts to always-on for exactly the entries that have had a recent contested change, rather than for every entry equally.

What this locks in

  • #17 every fact needs a resolvable, jumpable span regardless of which option wins here. Even "Nowhere" needs it internally, because C5's evidence popovers depend on it whether or not facts get their own browsing surface.
  • #18 locks in the badge language itself: author_kind renders as a flat, permanent, non-alarming badge in history and in any diff view, never as a persistent tint on the entry body. C1 keeps the only colour that means "not accepted yet".
  • Deferred to C9: whether a fact that an audit flag disagrees with gets a third visual state, on top of "extracted" and "evidence for an open proposal". This page assumes not, to keep the palette to three meanings, but it is a real option C9 might need.
  • Hard to undo: once "on demand" trains GMs that provenance is a click away rather than ambient, making it ambient later (Option C) is a much bigger visual change than it looks, because it has to fight the Relations panel for the same inch of aside.
