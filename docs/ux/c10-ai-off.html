Why this is a decision

Guardrail 4 makes the promise: "the AI switches off completely, and what remains is a good wiki." What it does not fix is the scope of the switch. SPEC §4.1 already gives each universe its own image_style_id and loremaster_description, which argues for a per-universe setting; the product's own pitch (§1, §2) leads with the copilot in every flow, which argues for something just as global as the pitch. Get the scope wrong and either a GM with a private homebrew and a shared derived reference universe has to trust both or neither, or a cautious GM has to commit the whole account before trying the product on one low-stakes world. Two smaller questions ride on the same choice: whether the history of what the Loremaster already wrote disappears with the switch, and what happens to whatever is already sitting in the review queue (C6) the moment it flips.

Issue #107 states the bar plainly: "with AI disabled, no model call is made." Every option below has to clear that, not just for the parts that visibly go quiet.

Options

A

Account-level switch

One setting above every universe. Off means off for all of them at once.

Buys

  • One switch, one truth: "no model call is made" is trivial to verify, there is only one place to check.
  • Nothing to explain: a GM who wants out gets out, in full, without picking a scope first.
  • Matches the pitch's own framing: the product leads with the copilot everywhere, so opting out means everywhere.

Costs

  • A private homebrew and a shared derived universe cannot disagree; the more sensitive one decides for both.
  • Trying the product cautiously on one universe before committing account-wide is not possible.
  • Turning it back on for one urgent propagation means turning it back on for everything.
B

Per-universe switch

Each universe carries its own Loremaster on/off, next to the style and voice fields §4.1 already gives it. Valdoria Reach and Sword Coast (ours) can disagree.

Buys

  • Matches the domain model already: a universe carries its own style and voice, its own AI posture is one more row, not a new concept.
  • A private homebrew and an AI-assisted campaign can sit in the same account without either compromising the other.
  • Still one switch per scope, so the guardrail stays checkable: "no model call for this universe" instead of "for this account".

Costs

  • Two universes, two mental models of what the product does; the GM has to remember which one they are in.
  • The setting lives one level away from the account page: auditing "is AI off everywhere" means checking every universe.
  • Nothing stops five universes from ending up in five states nobody remembers setting.
C

Per-feature switches

Ask, Complete, Propagate and Audit each get their own switch, plus image generation.

Buys

  • The most flexibility on paper: keep Ask, lose Propagate, without touching the rest.
  • Lets a cautious GM adopt the product one mode at a time instead of an all-or-nothing leap.
  • Nothing here the domain model resists: each mode already has its own trigger (§5's table).

Costs

  • Five switches is five states to verify against "no model call is made," most of which no GM will intentionally construct.
  • Undermines the guardrail's own bluntness: "off" stops meaning one thing, harder to state honestly in one sentence.
  • The combination most likely to actually get used: everything off is one click in A and B, five clicks here.

Rejected outright

An off switch that leaves warm pre-computation running quietly

Keeping §8.1's background triggers filling the warm cache while AI shows as "off" is tempting: turning it back on then feels instant, nothing stale. Guardrail 4's "switches off completely" carries no asterisk for background jobs, and issue #107 is explicit: no model call is made. A cache still being filled while the GM believes nothing is happening is the same trust break C6 rejects for an accept-all button, aimed at the honesty of the switch instead of the honesty of a diff.

What I would take

B, per universe

Take B. Guardrail 4's promise is easiest to keep honest one scope smaller than the whole account, and Valdoria Reach next to Sword Coast (ours) is exactly the case the fixture world hands us: one homebrew world a GM might want untouched, one derived reference world where retrieval is the entire reason it was added. A gets the guarantee's simplicity right but forces one answer onto two universes with different reasons to want different answers; C buys flexibility nobody asked for at the cost of a promise that is supposed to be blunt.

What fills the space: the Ask button disappears and nothing replaces it: that surface is legitimately empty, not backfilled with a fake widget. The aside gains one thing: a manual "Add relation" control, because with the Loremaster off, typing a relation goes back to being the form SPEC §4.2 otherwise avoids by inferring the type and confirming it in one click. That is the honest cost of "still a good wiki," not a detail to gloss over.

History and the inbox: the entry's revision history (human vs ai_accepted, guardrail 2) is not part of the switch: it is the factual record of what the entry already contains, and hiding it would falsify the entry's own past. Whatever sits in the queue when the switch flips stays exactly there: no new proposals get generated, since every trigger in §5's table stops calling a model, but nothing already generated is silently discarded either. An automatic bulk-reject on toggle would be the accept-all button's mirror image, a default nobody asked for, so C6's own queue is the only way that inbox empties, on or off.

Cheapest way to find out this is wrong: watch whether GMs who turn a universe off come back asking for just Ask, the one mode that only reads. If that is the recurring request, it argues for splitting Ask out as its own switch on top of B, not for adopting C wholesale.

What this locks in

  • #107 lands the switch on the universe, and "no model call is made" gets verified per universe, matching how it will actually be tested.
  • Once GMs learn "off" means one clean switch per universe, adding per-feature granularity later has to sit underneath that switch, not replace it: a universe-level off must keep meaning everything off even after finer controls exist.
  • Deferred: per-feature switches (option C), if usage ever justifies the complexity. Not decided here, and no current decision id owns it yet.
