Every screen Canonry has to grow, and every decision that has to be taken before it does

Written before there was any UI, which is what made the decisions below free: each one was about to be taken by accident by whoever implemented the first issue that needed it. The UI exists now, so read the product pass next for what actually shipped.

What this is

One page per decision. Each page states the question, renders two to four options as working mock UI on the same sample world, lists what each option buys and costs, names the option I would take and says what the choice locks in downstream. The options are drawn rather than described because half of these questions cannot be answered from a paragraph: density, marking, diff layout and phone ergonomics are decided by looking.

All seven rounds are decided: 64 answers, the first 49 on 2026-08-13, H1 and H2 on 2026-08-14, round four's ten and round five's one on 2026-08-15, and round six's and round seven's on 2026-08-16, recorded in DECISIONS.md and in the register below, so a page you open shows what was chosen at the top and keeps its options and its rejected section underneath. Five of those answers went against what the artifact recommended, which is the set working rather than failing: A1 took the reading room and repainted everything here, G2 took serif all the way down to the number columns, G9 kept retrieval running with the AI off, I1 kept a door page in the app rather than redirecting to sign-in, and I9 took shadcn-svelte for the whole control layer rather than six components of our own.

Round four came from the other direction. The first three answered questions asked before there was any code. Round four is what the shipped UI asked back: I signed into the deployed preview as a new user, could not create a universe, and audited every route to find out why. Its ten answers live in one artifact rather than ten, because it is a pass over the product rather than a question, the product pass, and they are filed as #135.

Round five has no artifact. #153, filed by round four as a defect rather than a decision, turned out to answer a bigger question once user profiles stopped being hypothetical: what the top-level URL segments mean. That is J1, recorded directly in DECISIONS.md rather than drawn as options first, filed as epic #156.

Round six has no artifact either. The product had already answered "are relation types fixed or free" twice in code, and differently: free in the schema and the import tool, fixed in the shipped catalogue and the migrations that keep extending it, invisible in the UI either way. That is K1, free labels reconciled by a resolver before anything writes and a human on the write, recorded directly in DECISIONS.md, filed as epic #188.

  1. Every page in the register shows what was chosen at the top, and keeps its options and its rejected section underneath, because the reasoning behind an option that lost is what stops it being reopened.
  2. To change a decision, edit DECISIONS.md and the register in assets/ux.js together, and say so on the issues it blocks.
  3. A new question goes in as its own artifact, in the same shape, rather than as a paragraph in an issue nobody reads twice.

Guardrails 1 to 7 are constraints on all of this, not preferences. Where an obvious design breaks one, it appears on the page under "rejected outright" with the guardrail number, so the reason survives the decision and nobody reopens it in six months. One decision in round two, G6, exists because two accepted answers disagree about where guardrail 1's edge actually is.

The state of the UI today

The paragraph below was true when it was written and is not true now. The application shipped: forty-seven routes, the entry and its editor, the whole propagation loop, the import surfaces, Ask, table mode, the players' wiki, the admin panels and both palettes. What the decisions did not produce is the ring around those screens, which is why signing into the preview still feels like an internal tool. The product pass audits what the deployment actually serves, surface by surface, and puts two or three drawn options on each of the ten places it falls down. This page says what was decided; that one says what was built.

Written on 2026-08-13: two commits, no application code, 110 issues on the board and none of them started. SPEC.md fixes the stack (SvelteKit 2, Svelte 5 runes, Tailwind 4, shadcn-svelte) and the domain model, and it is deliberately quiet about the interface: it says a proposal shows its evidence, not where the evidence sits; it says AI text is visually distinct, not what distinct looks like; it says nothing at the table waits on a model, not what replaces a spinner.

The inventory below maps every visible surface in the product to the issues that build it and the decisions that gate it. Most engine issues (schema, matching, the import loop, indexing) appear nowhere in it, which is the point: they can start today. A surface can start once its gate is answered, and after round one nearly all of them are: the shell, the entry and its editor, relations, revisions, the whole propagation loop, the import surfaces, onboarding and the landing page.

Five are still waiting on a round-two answer rather than on a designer: the palette shortcuts (G3), how export is presented (G10), the reveal timing and the players wiki (G7), what the meter does with the AI off (G9), and how often generation confirms a spend (G11). DECISIONS.md lists both sets.

The decisions

The review rubric

Every screen in the inventory gets checked against these before its issue moves to Done. They are the guardrails restated as questions somebody can answer while looking at a pull request.

1. Propose, never apply

Is there any path where model output lands in canon without a human pressing accept on that specific entry? Is there an accept-all anywhere it is not explicitly permitted?

2. AI text is distinct

Can a GM tell proposed text from their own at a glance, in both palettes, without relying on colour alone? Is accepted AI text still traceable a month later?

3. Evidence, never a score

Does every proposal name the entry and the sentence it came from? Is there a percentage anywhere pretending to be a reason?

4. The AI switches off

With the copilot off, is this screen still coherent, or is it a shell with a hole in it?

5. Data transparency

At the moment content leaves for a provider, can the GM find out which one and what happens to it, in a sentence rather than a policy?

6. Nothing unreviewed reaches players

Can anything on this screen reach the players wiki without an explicit reveal? Generated images included.

7. Never promise consistency

Does any string here claim the canon is consistent, verified, or coherent? Detection sits around F1 52%, so the honest verb is "looks".

The table test

If this screen can appear during play, does anything on it wait on a model? Instant means under 100 ms from the graph.

Not decided here

Three things are open in the spec itself and no artifact in this set can close them, because they are measurements or procurement rather than design: which wikis get indexed first, which metered provider backs production imports, and the matching thresholds of section 6.4, which come out of a benchmark. Two more are out of scope by the spec's own decision: anything in section 13's exclusion list (Foundry sync, system profiles, a Discord bot, player writing, native apps) and the v2 distribution stub.

What is also deliberately absent: a component library audit. shadcn-svelte decides most of the small parts, and there is no point choosing a button until the screens that need buttons exist.
