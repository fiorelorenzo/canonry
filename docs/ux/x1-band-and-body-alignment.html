Why this is a decision

V1 = B settled two things in one answer and never said how they relate. The band is rendered as the first thing a route draws, with no wrapper around it, so it sits against main's own gutter; the body is wrapped in mx-auto max-w-reading or max-w-working, so it centres inside that same gutter. Both halves do exactly what V1 says. What nothing says is where the title of a page is in relation to the first line of its prose, and the answer the code currently gives is "it depends", by up to 336 pixels.

The issue that filed this measured three reading routes and found the same 336 everywhere. That reading is right and it is not the whole shape, which is the first thing to fix before choosing. The offset is not a constant. It is max(0, (available − cap) / 2), where available is the viewport minus the sidebar minus the two gutters and cap is the body width the page declared. So it varies with the width a page picked, with whether that route sits inside the app shell or outside it, and with the viewport, and it takes six distinct values between 0 and 336. Measured on this branch at four widths over twelve routes:

Three consequences follow, and each one changes an option's argument rather than decorating it. Every wide route already agrees, at every width, because a body with no cap starts where the band starts: seven of the thirty-one call sites are already the thing option A would make universal. Nothing disagrees below 1280, on any route, because the cap stops biting before the gutter does. And the nineteen working routes disagree by 64 at 1440 and by nothing at 1280, which means option B as the issue words it, flush left on reading pages, leaves nineteen routes still misaligned at desktop width and closes four.

Two more measurements worth having before looking at anything. The band's own description is already capped at --container-measure, 544px, while its h1 is flush: the band is halfway to a measure already, in one component, by an earlier decision nobody wrote down as this one. And the reason this surfaced on /u/<handle> rather than anywhere else is arithmetic: /privacy's body is 3986px of ink and /u/lorenzo's is 104px in a 900px viewport, so on the profile the geometry is the page.

Guardrail 4 the AI switches off completely, and what remains is a good wiki. With the copilot off, the reading surface is the entire product, so its geometry is not a finish: it is the thing being sold. That is the only guardrail this question touches, and it does not settle it, because all three options below leave a readable wiki. What it does rule out is deciding this by accident inside a component, which is what will happen on the next route somebody builds.

Options

Each option is drawn on two pages: /privacy, where the offset exists today and nobody has ever noticed it, and /u/lorenzo, where it is the whole complaint. Both sheets are the same to-scale 1440×900 viewport with no sidebar, which is the largest case and the one both of those routes actually render in. An option that only looked right on the profile would be arguing dishonestly, so the long page comes first in each.

Checked rather than asserted: measured in the rendered page, the C sheets put the offset at 335 design pixels and the column at 702 against the app's own 336 and 704, and the A and B sheets put it at 0. The 1px is the caps being percentages of a scaled box, and it is the whole error budget of every mock below.

A

The band centres its content on the body's width

The band keeps its full-width paper and its rule; what moves is its content, onto the same cap the page declared for its body. A title sits above its own prose at every width.

Buys

  • The offset becomes 0 by construction at every width, every viewport, signed in or out, on all thirty-one call sites, rather than at the four the issue measured.
  • It is the only option under which a reader can learn the relationship once. The other two leave it varying with the page.
  • Nothing moves on a reading band except the title, the eyebrow and the description, because none of the five reading call sites carries actions or filters: that half of the band is empty today.
  • It finishes a rule the band already half-follows, since the description is capped at 544px while the title is not.

Costs

  • The band has to know the width, and today it cannot: PageBody owns that prop. Either both components take it, at all thirty-one call sites, and a route can then silently disagree with itself, or the two collapse into one Page component with one width and a header snippet, which is also thirty-one files but leaves one source of truth. The honest version of A is the second, and it ends PageHeader and PageBody moving independently.
  • Two bands' actions move inward at 1440: the entry page's write controls and audit badge by 64px, and /dev/ui's by the same. The three wide bands that carry actions do not move at all.
  • Every page in the app looks slightly different on the same day, which is a screenshot-set and a preview-eyeball cost even though it is one commit.
  • At 1440 a reading title indented 368px from the left of a 1440 sheet is a real change in character: a wide sheet with a narrow document on it, rather than a page with a header.
B

The body goes flush left on reading pages

The band does not move. PageBody's reading branch drops mx-auto, so the column starts where the title starts.

Buys

  • One line in one component, and no call site changes at all. Genuinely the cheapest thing on this page.
  • PageHeader and PageBody stay independent, which is worth something on its own: the width stays a fact about the body, which is what V1 says it is.
  • It is trivially reversible, so it is the option you can take, look at on the preview for a week, and undo without a migration of thirty-one files.

Costs

  • It does not close the gap. It fixes five reading call sites and leaves the nineteen working ones offset by 64px at 1440. Extending it to working is a different and much larger decision, and it is in the rejected section below.
  • It gives up the centred reading column, which is most of what A1 = B's reading room is about at desktop width: warm paper with a measure on it. A 704px column pinned to the left of a 1376px sheet is a documentation site, not a reading room.
  • It makes the page the issue was filed about worse rather than better, which is the strongest single argument against it, and the mock above is the argument.
  • It leaves the band's description capped at 544 while the column beside it is 704 and both are flush, so the band still does not agree with itself.
C

It stays, and V1 says so on purpose

No code changes. V1 gains a sentence: the band is a full-width frame that belongs to the route, not a header over the column, so the next person who measures this finds an answer instead of a defect.

Buys

  • Zero code, zero risk, and every screenshot in docs/ux and every preview eyeball stays valid.
  • The band stays free to hold whatever a route needs at full width, which is the reason V1 rejected option C in round seventeen (a filter row and a search field against a 48px shell bar), and that argument does not weaken with the page's width.
  • It is the only option that costs nothing before a beta, and the finding is a P3.

Costs

  • The sentence being added is not quite true as the issue words it. A frame at a constant distance from its content is a frame; this one sits 0, 64, 128, 192, 208, 256 or 336 pixels away depending on the route, the shell and the viewport. Writing it down means writing down that variance, or writing down something the measurement contradicts.
  • It leaves /u/<handle> looking like a defect to everyone who has not read DECISIONS.md, including the beta users, who will file it again.
  • It closes the question without answering it, so the next surface with little ink, and there will be one, reopens it.

Rejected outright

Narrowing main itself so the band inherits the width

The obvious cheap route to A: put the cap on main and let both children sit inside it, no props, no call sites, one place. It regresses #527 in the way that took a day to find the first time. The band bleeds -mx-8 and re-adds px-8 precisely so its paper reaches the edge of the scrollport, because it is sticky and content scrolls behind it; a bleed only reaches its parent's padding box, so with a narrowed main the band's paper stops at the column and content scrolls past on both sides of it. Worse, it makes wide impossible: the entries table and table mode need the whole shell, and they would inherit whatever cap main carried.

B extended to working and wide, so nothing is ever centred

It is the only version of B that actually makes the offset 0 everywhere, and that is exactly why it has to be named and refused here rather than arrived at later as "B, but consistently". It repeals the reading-room measure app-wide, not on five routes: an entry at working pinned to the left of a 1376px sheet, R1's portrait figure with no margin to sit in, and the paper that A1 = B chose becoming a background rather than a page. If Lorenzo wants that, it is a new question about A1 with its own artifact, not a variant of this one.

What I would take

A, and I would merge the two components rather than add the prop twice

A is the only option that makes the number 0 rather than smaller, and the measurement is what turns that from a preference into an argument: the offset has six values across the app today, so there is nothing for a reader to learn and nothing for a reviewer to check against. A gives them one rule, on all thirty-one call sites, in the same commit. The two facts that make it cheaper than it looks are that no reading band carries actions or filters, so the only thing that moves on those five routes is the title, and that all seven wide routes are already aligned, so a third of the app does not move at all.

The version I would build is one Page component taking width once, with a header snippet, rather than the same prop on both halves: thirty-one files either way, and only one of the two makes a route unable to contradict itself. That is a real loss of independence between PageHeader and PageBody and I would take it, because the independence is what produced this.

The cheapest way to find out I am wrong: A on the two routes in the mocks above, on the preview, for a day. If a 368px indent on a title reads as a document rather than as a page that lost its header, it is right; if it reads as the second, C is the honest answer and B never was, because B makes the page this was filed about worse.

What this locks in

  • A: #494's PageHeader and PageBody become one component with one width, all thirty-one call sites change in one commit, and the band's actions and filters rows are from then on the width of the body rather than the width of the shell. Every existing screenshot in docs/ux that shows a reading or working route is out of date the same day.
  • B: the reading room stops meaning a centred measure at desktop width, and the nineteen working routes keep a 64px offset that nothing on record explains. The next person to measure it files this issue again with a smaller number.
  • C: V1 grows a sentence that has to name the variance rather than claim a constant frame, and /u/<handle> ships as it is. Anything later that wants a title over its own column becomes a per-route exception, which is the thing V1 exists to prevent.
  • Either way this is a V1 amendment applied everywhere at once, not per route, and it is recorded in DECISIONS.md plus the register in assets/ux.js before any component moves.
