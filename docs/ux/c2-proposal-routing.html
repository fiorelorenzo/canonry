Why this is a decision

Propagation is debounced and runs in the background, so by the time seven proposals exist the GM has usually saved, closed the entry and moved on to something else. The spec fixes what a proposal is; it says nothing about where it waits to be read. Pick the wrong place and either the GM never learns anything arrived, or every save turns into a pop-up they have to dismiss before they can keep typing.

Guardrail 1 propose, never apply, per entry, no accept-all default. Routing is not just where a proposal is displayed, it is what stops "seven things arrived" from turning into a single button that accepts all seven. Wherever proposals live, the smallest addressable unit stays one entry.

Volume varies by two orders of magnitude between the two producers of proposals: propagation caps at ten per run, import (D4) can land hundreds in one batch. A routing answer that works for ten and silently breaks for two hundred is not an answer, it is a demo.

Options

A

Inbox

A top-level nav place, "Proposals", with a count badge. Arrival is a quiet toast; the inbox itself groups by what triggered the run.

Buys

  • Import's hundreds and propagation's tens sit in separate rows with separate reviewers, never merged into one list to skim.
  • A persistent count is the same vocabulary as every inbox the GM already knows; nothing new to learn.
  • Works the same whether the GM is at the table, in an entry, or away entirely; it is a place, not an overlay.

Costs

  • One more click than a drawer to actually see the list; the GM has to leave whatever entry they are on.
  • An empty inbox is a page nobody visits, so it has to earn a nav slot even on a quiet day.
  • The toast is still a second surface to design and get right; the inbox alone does not solve arrival.
B

Drawer

A bell in the topbar; new proposals make it pulse. Clicking it slides a drawer over whatever the GM is looking at, without navigating away.

Buys

  • Zero navigation cost: review starts from wherever the GM already is.
  • The peek tab can pulse quietly instead of a toast that times out and is missed.
  • Closing the drawer costs nothing; nothing is lost by ignoring it for a session.

Costs

  • A drawer sliding over the exact entry the GM is mid-sentence in is the interruption the spec explicitly wants avoided.
  • Import's hundreds cannot fit a 280px drawer; it becomes a single link out, which makes the drawer half a solution.
  • No persistent place to point someone at; "where do I see old proposals" has no url.
C

Inline with a global counter

No queue anywhere. Each affected entry carries its own chip; the topbar shows a total count with no click-through list.

Buys

  • Zero new surfaces: no toast, no drawer, no page. The lightest possible build.
  • The chip is exactly where the GM would look anyway, on the entry itself.
  • Cannot ever interrupt mid-sentence, because nothing ever appears unprompted.

Costs

  • No way to review a run as a batch; a seven-entry propagation means visiting seven entries by memory or luck.
  • Import's hundreds have no home at all under this model except one chip per affected entry, which fails D4 outright.
  • A chip on the entry the GM is currently editing is its own small interruption: their own future is peeking at them mid-sentence.

Rejected outright

Auto-accept a proposal if it sits unopened for a while

Framed as "reduce inbox clutter", tempting once an inbox holds import's hundreds next to propagation's tens. It is a straightforward Guardrail 1 violation: an accept that happens because nobody clicked reject in time is not an accept, it is an apply with extra steps, and there is still no git diff to recover the entry afterwards.

What I would take

A, with B's arrival signal

Take A, the inbox, because it is the only option that gives import's hundreds and propagation's tens genuinely separate rows rather than forcing one surface to serve both scales. Borrow B's arrival pattern rather than a modal or an always-shown toast: a quiet badge increment on the nav item, plus a toast that behaves like a notification, not a dialog: auto-dismissing, never blocking input, never appearing while the entry it targets has unsaved changes open in the editor. That answers the interruption question directly: nothing pops over a sentence in progress, because nothing pops over anything; the badge is just there next time the GM looks up.

For a proposal that targets an entry the GM currently has open and unsaved: it is held out of view entirely, not rendered onto the live editor. It becomes visible in the inbox and on that entry the next time the GM opens it after their own edit is saved, so an in-progress sentence and an unreviewed AI rewrite of the same paragraph can never collide on screen at once.

Cheapest way to find out this is wrong: log how long a propagation batch sits unopened in the inbox against how long an import batch does. If propagation batches go stale for days, the badge is too quiet and needs the toast to persist longer or repeat; if GMs open the inbox constantly out of anxiety, it is too loud.

What this locks in

  • #47 the inbox's rows are a direct read of the proposal table filtered on a null outcome; "arrived" is not a separate event to track, it is a query, matching how the spec already frames the accept rate as instrumentation rather than an addition.
  • #51 the inbox lists proposals, it does not accept them; opening a row always lands on the per-entry diff, never a bulk action from the list itself.
  • Hard to undo: once import and propagation have separate rows in the same inbox, merging them later to "simplify" re-opens the batching problem this page solves.
  • Deferred on purpose: whether any bulk action exists at all on the inbox (select several, still review each, but move faster) belongs to C6, which owns where bulk is allowed without becoming accept-all.
