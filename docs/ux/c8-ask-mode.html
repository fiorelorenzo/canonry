Why this is a decision

SPEC §5 nails Ask down to the API: query_lore takes one to five questions in parallel, top-k 8, similarity threshold 0.5, sources listed rather than cited inline, follow-up questions, SSE streaming, five detail levels. None of that is open. What is open is where the GM meets any of it. Next to the entry they are already reading, through the same palette A3 gives every other quick action, or on a page of its own: and three smaller questions that ride on the same choice: how a source list avoids becoming a wall of links nobody opens, how five discrete levels avoid turning into a settings dialog, and, sharpest of the three, how an answer in a derived universe tells the GM which part came from their own canon and which part came from the pre-indexed corpus underneath it.

Get the last one wrong and the Loremaster starts sounding like it knows things about the GM's world that are actually someone else's setting: the exact failure SPEC §4.1 already guards against by making the user's canon always win. Ask is the surface where that promise either shows up or does not.

Guardrail 3 every proposal shows its evidence, never a bare confidence score. Ask never writes anything, so nothing here gets accepted: but the same rule is what stands between "sources listed" and a plausible-sounding answer with nothing under it.

Options

A

Panel beside the entry

A tab in the aside column, next to Proposals. The entry being asked about stays on screen the whole time.

Buys

  • The GM stays on Aldric Vane while asking about Aldric Vane; nothing breaks the reading flow.
  • Sources sit one scroll from the sentence they relate to, so guardrail 3 reads naturally instead of as a footnote.
  • Reuses the aside column C2 already claims for Proposals: Ask is one more tab, not new chrome.

Costs

  • A narrow column is a tight fit for a streamed answer, a source list, five level buttons and follow-ups without scrolling.
  • Only available where an entry is already open; asking something with no obvious "home" entry has nowhere to start.
  • A GM who fires all five parallel questions at once gets a cramped column fast.
B

Command palette

The same shortcut A3 already gives search and navigation. Type a question, the answer grows inside the overlay.

Buys

  • One shortcut for search, navigation and Ask: nothing new to learn once A3 exists.
  • Works from anywhere, including a blank entries list, with no entry required as context.
  • Invites a short question and a short answer, which suits the "1 line" and "Short" levels well.

Costs

  • A palette is built to close the moment you are done; a streamed answer with sources and follow-ups wants to stay open, fighting the overlay's own instinct.
  • Five level buttons plus a source list stop looking like a palette and start looking like a second app.
  • Reading paragraphs over a dimmed floating panel is a worse surface than the page underneath it.
C

Full page

A dedicated route. Room for the answer, the sources and the follow-ups without squeezing any of them.

Buys

  • Room to do sources, levels and follow-ups properly, all visible without stacking on each other.
  • A natural home for a longer session: several follow-ups read like a small transcript.
  • Derived-universe attribution has space to be a real visual difference, not a cramped icon.

Costs

  • Asking about the entry you are reading means leaving it: the flow break this whole page exists to avoid.
  • Heaviest of the three for the common case, which is closer to "what's this place called" than a research session.
  • A dedicated route implies Ask is a top-level destination ahead of A2's decision about what those even are.

Rejected outright

Inline numbered citations with a confidence percentage

The familiar chatbot shape (…answers to the Ashen Ledger[1][2], 87% confident", is tempting because every reader has seen it before. SPEC §5 already rules out the citations ("sources listed rather than cited inline"), and the confidence number is a straight breach of Guardrail 3: never an arbitrary confidence score. A percentage in place of the two source cards above tells the GM less, not more, and dresses a guess as a measurement.

What I would take

A, panel beside the entry, with B as its launcher

Take A. Most questions are about what the GM is already looking at, and a narrow column is a fixable scrolling problem, while B and C both cost something structural: B's overlay fights a long-lived answer, C's page breaks the flow of the entry you were just reading. B is not wasted: the same shortcut keeps opening the palette, but once a question has real content it routes into the panel rather than staying in the overlay, so A and B are an entry point and a destination, not two rivals. Five detail levels ship as the button row drawn above, never a settings dialog, because five discrete states are exactly what a row of buttons is for.

Cheapest way to find out this is wrong: watch panel-width complaints and follow-up depth. If GMs routinely open a second question before finishing the first one's follow-ups, that is the signal the panel is too small, and C earns its place as an "expand" affordance out of the same panel, not a separate rebuild.

What this locks in

  • #53 ships as the panel described here: the five-level row and follow-up chips are the shipped affordance, not a later iteration.
  • #60 gets its minimum bar from the two source-card shapes above: a first-party card (entry name, span) and a derived-corpus card (badge, source name, licence, link) that survives being narrow.
  • Once source cards ship in this shape, every other Loremaster mode that emits evidence: audit (C9), revision provenance (B4) inherits it rather than inventing a second one.
  • Deferred: whether the palette shortcut that opens Ask is the same binding search and navigation use, or its own key, is A3's decision. This page only decides what happens once a question has content.
