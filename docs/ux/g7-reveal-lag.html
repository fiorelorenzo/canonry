Why this is a decision

Two decisions and one spec sentence collide here without ever contradicting each other on paper. E5 chose the session log: nothing required during play, a pre-populated checklist confirmed in one pass after the table breaks. E7 chose the honest gap for anything the party has heard of but not yet discovered. SPEC 10 describes the result in five words: "if it came up at the table, it shows up." Nobody wrote down how long "shows up" is allowed to take, and E5's own cost list already named the number: the confirmation is a ritual a tired GM can skip, which means the wiki lags the table by however long that GM takes to open the checklist, and that can be the rest of the night, the following week, or never.

The fixture makes the gap concrete. Session 7 runs the night of 2026-07-30. Mid-session the party corners Aldric Vane, who names the Ashen Ledger twice; Corvin Ashe comes up once in passing. The table breaks around 22:30. A player who was there checks the wiki on the drive home, at 23:00. What they see there, and what they see if the GM's phone stays asleep on the nightstand until Thursday, is the actual content of this decision, not an abstraction about "eventual consistency".

Guardrail 6 nothing unreviewed is ever published to players. E5's confirmation step is what makes the session log satisfy this guardrail at all: the GM's pass over the checklist is the review. The question below is not whether to keep that review, it is how long a real table is willing to let it sit undone before the guardrail's own honesty, "if it came up, it shows up", stops being true in practice.

Options

A

The wiki simply lags

E5 and E7 exactly as decided, no added mechanism. Nothing publishes until the GM opens the end-of-session checklist and confirms it, whenever that happens.

At 23:00 a player pulls up Valdoria on their phone. The page is identical to how it looked this morning: the Ashen Ledger mention still routes to the gap page, "heard, not yet discovered." Nothing about tonight is visible anywhere, not even a note that a session happened at all. If the GM never opens the checklist, that stays true forever: the page does not expire or fall back to a default, it simply never catches up. SPEC 10's own words are true in the data model and false at every clock time between the table breaking and the GM's next free evening.

Buys

  • Nothing new to build: E5 and E7 exactly as decided, no added surface, no new schema.
  • One review pass a night, at the GM's own pace, is genuinely the least effortful design for someone tired after a real session.
  • No risk of a half-reviewed reveal reaching players early: the whole session is atomic, all or nothing, until confirmed.

Costs

  • A player who was at the table and heard a name said aloud finds nothing about it on the wiki that night, or the next day, or possibly ever.
  • "If it came up at the table, it shows up" is only true once the GM remembers a separate, optional step; nothing in the product enforces or even reminds.
  • A GM who never opens the checklist gets no signal that anything is waiting, so the gap is invisible to the one person who could close it.
B

Confirm before leaving the table

Same batch as E5, with a one-tap phone prompt offered the moment the GM ends the session, so the natural motion is confirm, then close the app, not a separate step for later.

At 23:00 the player sees Valdoria updated, because the GM confirmed at 22:34 on the way out the door. If the GM instead swipes the prompt away, closes the app, and gets busy for a week, the page reverts to option A's exact state and stays there until someone opens the checklist by hand. The phone prompt is a nudge, not a requirement, so its failure mode is A's failure mode, not a new one.

Buys

  • Closes the lag to minutes for the whole session in one motion, without inventing a second reveal path.
  • The prompt arrives at the moment the GM is already reaching for their phone to close things down, the cheapest possible moment to ask for one more tap.
  • Still just one model to explain: everything from tonight's session becomes visible when the GM confirms, full stop.

Costs

  • A GM who dismisses the prompt, or whose table runs past the point where anyone wants to look at a phone, falls straight back to A's unbounded lag, so the guarantee is a nudge, not a rule.
  • Nothing publishes mid-session even for the thing the party is clearly going to talk about on the way home; the wiki still cannot answer a question asked during a break at 22:00.
  • A single wrong tap, "confirm 3" when one of the three should have stayed private a while longer, publishes all three at once; there is no partial undo once the phone screen is dismissed.
C

Live for what is tapped, log for the rest

The optional one-tap flag E5 already kept during play actually publishes, immediately, entity-grain. Anything not tapped live still waits for the end-of-session batch.

At 23:00 the player sees a split result: the Ashen Ledger, tapped live at 20:14, is already a working link to the revealed entry, no lag at all. Corvin Ashe, mentioned once but never tapped, still routes to a gap page, exactly as in option A, until the GM reaches the end-of-session batch. If the GM never confirms that batch, the Ashen Ledger stays visible, since it already published the moment it was tapped, but Corvin Ashe never surfaces. Guardrail 6 holds either way, since a person acted on every fact that ever reached the players, live tap or later confirm; only the timing splits into two speeds.

Buys

  • The thing players actually want, being able to look something up minutes after hearing it, works for whatever the GM chose to flag live.
  • The remainder still gets a batch review, so guardrail 6 is intact end to end: nothing reaches players without some GM action, live or confirmed.
  • Reuses E5's own decided grain and interaction, entity-level, one tap; only what the tap does changes, not the model of what a reveal is.

Costs

  • Two publish paths instead of one means two things to explain, to a GM and to whoever builds the invalidation logic behind the public view.
  • A GM mid-scene, hands full running the table, is exactly the person least likely to remember an optional tap exists, so the live half may simply go unused, complexity bought for nothing.
  • Players can no longer tell, from the wiki alone, whether "nothing new here yet" means "nothing happened" or "something happened and the GM has not confirmed it": the live path makes the non-live remainder's silence more conspicuous, not less.

Rejected outright

Publish automatically from what session_context pinned

The moment an entity is pinned or mentioned during a scene, push it live, no GM action at all. It would close the lag to zero, which is exactly why it is tempting once you have spent a whole page on how bad the lag can get. It also removes the one thing standing between "the product thinks this came up" and "this is now public": a human's own judgement. Corvin Ashe getting pinned by a passing mention the GM meant to gloss over, then appearing on the players' wiki before the GM has said a word about him on purpose, is precisely what Guardrail 6 exists to prevent. The tap or the confirm, whichever option above keeps it, has to stay a person's decision, not session_context's.

What I would take

C, with B's nudge covering the remainder

Take C. E5's own mock already drew the hook: "flagged twice during play" implies the tap during the scene is doing something, and today it only pre-fills a checklist nobody is forced to open. Wiring it to actually publish, entity-grain, the same grain E5 already chose, costs nothing E5 has not already built, and it is the only one of the three that gets anything to a player's phone in the seconds after Aldric says the name, which is when a player actually wants to look something up, not three days later.

The remainder, whatever the GM never tapped live, still needs somewhere to land, and leaving it on A's open-ended lag undoes half the point. Fold in B's nudge for that part: the same one-tap "confirm and end session" prompt, offered right as the GM closes the app, so the untapped remainder lags minutes, not days, and only degrades to A's failure mode if the GM actively dismisses the prompt rather than simply forgetting a separate step exists.

Cheapest way to find out this is wrong: instrument the live tap across a handful of real sessions before building the publish wiring behind it. If GMs barely use the optional flag today, mid-scene, because running the table already fills both hands, the live half of C is complexity nobody exercises, and the honest answer collapses to B alone: one path, nudged, minutes instead of days.

What this locks in

  • #82 "revelation model tied to sessions" currently records what and when at the session grain. A live tap needs its own timestamp, the moment during play it happened, not just which session it belongs to, and it needs to record how the reveal happened, tapped live or confirmed after, since the confirmation surface has to show the difference honestly rather than presenting every reveal as if it arrived the same way.
  • #83 "read-only public view filtered by revelations" can no longer assume one batch write at the end of a session invalidates the players' wiki. A live tap means the public view has to reflect a single new revelation mid-session, a smaller and more frequent update than the issue's current shape implies.
  • #85 "no unrevealed content leaks in the API" needs a new case that only exists once a live path does: with a session still open, confirm that a live-tapped entity is visible while everything else pinned in that same open session, tapped or not, stays absent. Today's test only has an end-of-session boundary to check against; C adds a mid-session one.
  • Hard to undo: once players learn some reveals arrive live, mid-session, that expectation does not un-teach itself. Pulling the live tap back out later, if C turns out unused, is a visible regression, not a quiet simplification.
  • Defers: whether the live tap should cover facts rather than whole entities was E5's call, not this one, and it stays entity-grain here on purpose, matching what E5 already decided.
