Canonryproduct pass 2026-08-15 · UX audit · decisions · board

Forty-seven routes ship, every UX question is answered, and the first three screens still read as a scaffold

I signed into preview.canonry.io as a new user and could not create a universe. That is the symptom. The cause is one structural thing, and once it is named, most of the rest of the list stops being a list of small ugly screens and becomes one piece of work.

Ten surfaces, two or three drawn options each. All ten answered on 2026-08-15 and recorded in DECISIONS.md as round four. Each surface shows its answer under the question and keeps every option underneath, because the reasoning behind an option that lost is what stops it being reopened. Two went against my recommendation, I1 and I9, and both say so where they sit.

What this is, and how I read it

The decision set in index.html was written when there was no application code. It asked what the product should look like and answered fifty-one questions. This page is the other direction: I opened the deployed preview as somebody who had never seen it, wrote down every place it felt like an internal tool, and then read every route in apps/web/src/routes to find out whether what I felt was a missing decision, a decision taken and not built, or a defect.

Almost none of it is a missing decision. The visual language is decided and implemented: routes/layout.css is a complete, careful design system with the reading room in both palettes, serif everywhere down to the number columns per G2, tabular figures and a 34rem reading measure. The seven-item sidebar A2 chose exists and is correct. The copilot loop, the import surfaces, table mode, the players' wiki and the admin panels are all built and most of them are good. What is missing is everything that holds those screens together, and the reason it is missing is that no issue owned it.

Guardrails 1 to 7 bind this page too. Nothing proposed here adds an accept-all, spends violet outside the copilot, or lets a string claim that a canon is consistent. Where an obvious idea breaks one, it is drawn and rejected with the number, so it does not come back.

The one diagnosis

The app shell exists only inside a universe. Sidebar.svelte is mounted by routes/u/[universe]/+layout.svelte and by nothing else, so every route that is not under /u/{slug} renders as a bare <main class="mx-auto max-w-measure px-8 py-16"> floating on the paper, under a forty-pixel bar that holds one sentence and two text links.

That single fact produces most of the findings below. There is no way in, because the landing page is the empty app home. There is no way to create anything, because the create routes exist and nothing links to them. There is no account, because there is nowhere to hang one. The language switcher disappears the moment you sign in, because it lives on the two pages that have their own header. Settings is five islands. A new user's first screen after paying attention to a sign-up form is a sentence telling them they have nothing.

What follows from it. I3 is the same decision as I6 and half of I1: does the shell reach outside a universe, or does the product keep two kinds of page. Take that one first and six of the ten below collapse into it. The rest are genuinely separate: where the language control lives, what an empty state is for, whether an entry browser exists, whether the phone is a supported client, and whether anybody owns the buttons.

I1 · the door

The app's own front door, now that the landing page lives somewhere else

I started this section believing the landing page had never been built, and that is wrong: it ships from canonry-landing, a separate SvelteKit app on prodbox, and canonry.io serves F6's demo as the hero with G10's export sentence underneath it, in English at / and Italian at /it per issue #129. So the pitch is handled. What is not handled is what the application itself serves at / to somebody who is not signed in, which today is the empty universe list with a broken sentence in it.

decidedB, the door page. Taken against my recommendation of A, so the app keeps a signed-out root instead of redirecting to sign-in. The cost A avoided is now real and worth naming here: the sentence on this page and the pitch on canonry.io have to be kept in step by hand, and the app's copy is the one nobody will remember to update.

Today

  • The sentence is doubled. The link renders t.shell.signIn and the sentence after it is t.universe.list.signInPrompt, which already opens with the same two words, so the page reads "Sign in Sign in to see your universes." apps/web/src/routes/+page.svelte:23-26 against i18n/en.ts:955.
  • It is a page with no purpose. It is not the landing page, because that is a different deployment on a different domain, and it is not the sign-in page, because that is one link away. It exists only because / had to render something.
  • Appearance settings is the only outbound link. Not docs, not privacy, not pricing. Guardrail 5 lives on /privacy and nothing in the interface points at it.
  • The mark is not a link and there is no header. Landing, home and error state are all the same route with three branches inside one <main>.

Options

A

Redirect: the app has no signed-out home

Signed out at / is a redirect to /auth/sign-in, and the sign-in page carries one link back to canonry.io for anybody who arrived wanting to know what this is. The application never renders a pitch, so there is exactly one copy of the pitch and it lives in the repository whose job that is.

Buys

  • One line in the home loader, and the broken screen stops existing rather than being redesigned.
  • The pitch stays in one repository, so there is no second version of the guardrail 7 copy to keep in sync as the product changes.
  • Removes the only reason / has a signed-out branch at all, which also removes the doubled sentence with it.

Costs

  • Somebody who bookmarked the app and forgot what it was gets a password box with no explanation beyond one footer link.
  • A redirect is invisible in analytics as an intention: you cannot tell a curious visitor from a returning user, if that ever matters.
B

A door page: a sentence, two actions, the links that must exist

Keep the signed-out branch and make it a door rather than an empty room. The spec's own TL;DR sentence, which the layout already ships as its meta description and which has already been through guardrail 7, then sign in and create an account, then the links the product owes a visitor.

Buys

  • A bookmarked app domain still explains itself, and docs and privacy get their link without waiting for the shell work in I3.
  • Costs an afternoon and reuses copy that already exists.

Costs

  • Two copies of the pitch in two repositories, and the shorter one will be the one nobody remembers to update.
  • It is a page that exists to be passed through. Every visitor takes one more click than A gives them.
C

Sign-in is the root

Move the form itself to / for a signed-out visitor and keep /auth/sign-in as an alias. No redirect, no extra page, one address that renders the form when you are out and the universe list when you are in.

Buys

  • Fastest path to the only action a signed-out visitor can take, with no redirect hop.
  • One route to maintain instead of two, and the sign-in page is already the nicest screen in the product once I2 lands.

Costs

  • Two very different pages on one address, which is the shape that produced the current three-branch +page.svelte in the first place.
  • Deep links to /auth/sign-in exist in emails and in the codebase, so the alias is permanent rather than temporary.

One thing this surface does not fix. canonry.io currently offers a waiting list and no way into the app, which is right for a product that is not launched and wrong the morning it is. Whichever option wins here, launch day needs a Sign in link in the landing page's header, and that is a change in canonry-landing, not in this repository.

I would take A. The landing page exists, it is good, and it is already bilingual: the application has no business carrying a second, worse copy of the same argument, which is what B becomes within a month. C is tempting and I would rather not have two things at one address again, since that is exactly how today's page ended up with three branches and a duplicated sentence in one of them. A is one line in routes/+page.server.ts and it deletes a screen rather than designing one.

I2 · auth

Sign in and sign up, the first screen anybody actually sees

Both pages are a left-aligned column stuck to the top of an empty page, with the language toggle as the second most prominent thing on the screen and the submit button smaller than the fields above it. Everything on them works. Nothing on them says a person designed them.

decidedC for sign-in, B for sign-up. As recommended. One layout with an optional right pane: the door is the title page, and the screen where somebody decides to sign up carries the argument. The four defects in the flaw list are not part of the choice and go in the same commit.

Today

  • The language toggle is the loudest control on the page. Two pills at the top right, at the same level as the wordmark, on a screen whose only job is a password. routes/auth/sign-in/+page.svelte:71. See I5 for where it belongs.
  • The submit is w-fit. Two full-width fields and then a small button, so the primary action is the least prominent thing in the form. routes/auth/sign-in/+page.svelte:117-123.
  • Nothing is centred and nothing is bounded. A 34rem column pinned to the top left of a 1440px page, which is why the screenshot has a third of a screen of empty paper under it.
  • There is no password recovery anywhere in the product. Not a link, not a route. A forgotten password is currently a database query.
  • The error is a paragraph. Rendered into the flow with no role="alert" and no aria-live, so a screen reader is never told the sign-in failed. routes/auth/sign-in/+page.svelte:125-127.
  • The mark goes nowhere. On the one page where a visitor may want to find out what this product is before typing an email into it, the wordmark is not a link.

Options

A

The same column, done properly

One card, centred in the viewport, mark above the title and linking home, full-width primary, recovery link in the form, language demoted to a quiet footer row with docs and privacy. Nothing structural, one file each.

Buys

  • Cheapest thing on this page that fixes the actual complaint.
  • Recovery, docs and privacy all get their link, and the error can get its role="alert" in the same edit.

Costs

  • A centred card is the most generic screen in software. It is correct and it says nothing about this product.
B

Split: the form, and the reason to fill it in

The card on the left, and on the right the product doing its one trick on the sample world: a sentence changed, a proposal drafted, marked as unaccepted and waiting. Static, no model, no network. Below 900px the right pane drops and this becomes A.

Buys

  • The one screen where a stranger decides whether to bother now carries the reason.
  • It is honest about the guarantee at the exact moment the guarantee matters, which is the argument guardrail 1 exists to win.

Costs

  • A second layout to maintain, and a right pane that is marketing copy inside the app, so it needs the same guardrail 7 review the landing page gets.
  • On sign-in, where the visitor is already sold, the pane is noise.
C

The title page

Full paper, the mark set large and centred, the form at 22rem under it and nothing else above the fold. The reading room applied to the one screen that currently looks like every other login form on the internet.

Buys

  • It looks like this product and not like a template, using only what H2 already decided.
  • One layout serves sign-in, sign-up, recovery and any future verification screen.
  • Nothing on it needs a guardrail 7 review, because it makes no claim.

Costs

  • Makes no argument, so it leans on the landing page having done that work. Somebody who arrives directly at sign-up from a friend's link learns nothing.
  • Vertical centring on a short laptop viewport needs care or the footer rule pushes the form up awkwardly.

Guardrail 2 Rejected outright: an auth screen painted in the copilot's violet. It is the most tempting thing on the palette and the one colour these pages may not spend. C1 reserves violet for unaccepted AI text, and a product whose sign-in page is violet teaches every user that violet means Canonry rather than "a model wrote this and you have not accepted it". The mark itself follows the same rule and stays burnt umber, per H2.

I would take C for sign-in and B for sign-up. The door is quiet and the decision to sign up gets the argument, which is the same split F6 already made between the demo and the app. They share one layout: B is C with a right pane, so this is one component with a slot, not two designs. Whichever wins, the four defects in the flaw list are fixed in the same commit, since the recovery link and the role="alert" are not design questions. The board has the auth surface as Done, in #86, so this is a new issue rather than a reopened one.

I3 · home

The home page, signed in

This is the first screen anyone with an account actually lands on, and it has to say where the product is: your universes, and one way into a new one. Today it says one sentence and offers a settings link.

decidedB, the shell reaches outside a universe, with C's redirect as the zero-universe behaviour. This is the structural answer and the largest piece of work on the page: A2's sidebar stops being a universe frame and becomes the product's frame, with an account-level mode when no universe is selected. Six of the other nine surfaces get easier the day it lands.

Today

  • No CTA, zero universes. The empty state is a single sentence with nothing to press. apps/web/src/routes/+page.svelte:27-30, string at apps/web/src/lib/i18n/en.ts:956.
  • Doubled sentence, signed out. The unauthenticated state renders "Sign in" immediately followed by "Sign in to see your universes." apps/web/src/routes/+page.svelte:22-26.
  • No account chrome. The only link on the page is "Appearance settings"; no name, no email, no sign-out here, no Docs. apps/web/src/routes/+page.svelte:59-64; the only other chrome on the page is apps/web/src/lib/components/shell/AuthStatus.svelte:36-51, a name and a sign-out button.
  • Cards aren't cards, and there's no "New universe" even when they exist. The populated state is an unstyled <ul> of links with no create action anywhere on the page. apps/web/src/routes/+page.svelte:32-56.

Options

A

Fix the page

Same standalone page, still outside the shell, but a real one: a page header, universe cards that look like cards, a permanent primary "New universe" and a secondary "Import a world," and account chrome instead of a lone settings link.

Buys

  • Cheapest of the three: no shell rearchitecture, ships against the page that exists today.
  • "/" stays a simple, predictable route with no dependency on whether a universe is selected.

Costs

  • Invents a second chrome system: this page's header bar is not A2's sidebar, so Settings and Docs get built twice.
  • The account bar drawn here (name, Settings, Docs, sign out) has nowhere else to live; it doesn't carry theme, language, or the F2 quota meter, so those still need a home.
B

The shell reaches outside a universe

A2's sidebar becomes the frame for the whole product. With no universe selected, the switcher offers "All universes" and "New universe," the nav below it becomes the account's own places, and the same footer carries the user, theme, language and quota everywhere.

Buys

  • One frame for the whole product: signing in never drops anyone onto a chrome-less page again.
  • Theme, language and the quota meter get a permanent home instead of each needing its own answer per orphan route.
  • Reuses A2's component rather than adding a second nav pattern.

Costs

  • The sidebar now has two shapes (universe nav, account nav) instead of one, so it has to branch on whether a universe is selected.
  • More surface to build before this page ships: the footer (user, theme, language, meter) doesn't exist anywhere yet, in or out of a universe.
C

No home page at all

"/" redirects instead of rendering: zero universes goes to /onboarding, one universe goes straight into it, two or more shows a small chooser rather than a dashboard.

Buys

  • Kills the dead corner outright: nobody with zero or one universe ever sees an empty list or a picker they don't need.
  • Routes D7's decision to the exact moment a fresh account needs it, instead of leaving /onboarding for nobody to find.

Costs

  • A returning multi-universe user gets an extra screen between sign-in and their last universe, where a dashboard could remember and skip it.
  • Redirect logic now lives in +page.server.ts rather than being visible as a page, which is one more place a future universe-count edge case can go unnoticed.

I would take B, with C's redirect as the zero-universe behaviour. They compose rather than compete: B says what the page looks like when it exists, an account frame with no universe selected; C says when it shouldn't exist at all, so a fresh sign-in with nothing yet never renders that frame around an empty list, it goes straight to /onboarding. Locks in a universe-less mode for A2's sidebar (#104, the app shell) and gives Settings and Docs an actual place in the nav instead of a lone link in prose.

I4 · onboarding

Creating a universe

D7 decided the onboarding path: name a universe, then import first with a pre-indexed fallback. What shipped is that screen plus a second, undecided one, and neither is reachable from anywhere in the product.

decidedB, one creation surface. /onboarding absorbs /u/new, the three starts are cards with D7's import as the loud default, and /u/new redirects into it rather than staying a second door.

Today

  • Two paths, one decision. D7 chose import-first; apps/web/src/routes/onboarding/+page.svelte:1-81 builds it, but apps/web/src/routes/u/new/+page.svelte:22-38 ships a rival empty-universe form that never asks.
  • Neither is reachable. No route or button in the app links to /u/new or /onboarding, and nothing redirects a fresh account to either. apps/web/src/routes/+page.svelte:16-65 is the entire home page and contains no such link.

Options

A

Keep both and link them

/u/new stays the two-second empty universe, /onboarding stays the guided import path, and I3's home page links to both directly. No new screens, just two doors that finally open.

Buys

  • Smallest possible change: the two screens already exist and already work.
  • Ships this sprint, no new copy or layout to design.

Costs

  • Two entry points that diverge immediately, one asks for import, one doesn't, with nothing on the home page saying which is "the right one."
  • Every future third or fourth start (a template, a different pre-indexed base) needs its own new route rather than a card on an existing one.
B

One creation surface

/onboarding absorbs /u/new: name, then three starts drawn as cards, import a world, start empty, derive from a pre-indexed universe. /u/new redirects into it.

Buys

  • One place to build a fourth start into later, instead of a new route every time.
  • "Start empty" becomes an equal, visible option instead of the only thing /u/new does under a different name.
  • D7's import stays the loud default, badge and position both, without hiding the other two paths.

Costs

  • The page now holds three cards' worth of copy before anyone types a name, busier than either of today's two screens alone.
  • The onboarding route grows to own routing that used to be split between it and /u/new.
C

Create inline, choose the start afterward

A universe is created from the home page with a name and nothing else. The empty universe itself carries the three starts as its first screen, so the choice is made with the shell already around you.

Buys

  • The choice happens inside the product, with B's shell already around the user, instead of before it exists.
  • A name is genuinely all creation needs; nothing else is decided yet.

Costs

  • A universe now exists, uncounted and sourceless, before its content is decided, so "0 entries, no source chosen" becomes a state the data model and the sidebar's counts both have to represent.
  • Splits D7's decision across two routes again, just a different two than today's.

I would take B. One route absorbs the other, D7's import path is the loud default instead of the hidden one, and "start empty" stops being a secretly different flow living at its own URL. It locks in #108 as the only place a new start gets added, so a later fourth card doesn't need a fourth route, and it folds #26's job into onboarding's own import card rather than leaving two forms answering the same question.

I5 · language

Where the language switch lives, and why it is not a flag

Today the switcher exists on exactly the two pages where there is no account to save it to, and vanishes on the third. Sign in and the control is gone: the page that writes a real preference, /settings/language, is not linked from anywhere in the interface. So a GM who lands in English and wants Italian has to know the URL.

decidedB, the account menu, with Settings behind it. Endonyms, current value on the row. The flags are not happening, for the reasons in the costs above; if the top bar ever wants this control, it carries “Italiano” as text and not a country.

Today

  • The switcher is mounted on two routes. components/auth/LocaleSwitcher.svelte appears in routes/auth/sign-in/+page.svelte:71 and the sign-up page, and nowhere else in the application.
  • The page that saves the preference is unreachable. routes/settings/language writes a per-account preference, which SPEC 17 wants because it has to follow the GM to the phone at the table, and nothing links to it.
  • Two different mechanisms, no shared control. The auth pages write a cookie, the settings page writes the account, and a user who switches at sign-in has no way to see which of the two is in force.
  • On the auth pages it outranks the product. Sitting top right at the same weight as the wordmark, it is the second thing the eye lands on, for a control most accounts touch once.

The thing this control must not imply. Canonry has two language concepts and SPEC 17 keeps them apart on purpose: the interface and copilot language, which follows the person, and the canon language, which belongs to each entry and stays whatever it was written in. components/entry/LanguageControl.svelte already ships the second one, next to the entry's type. There is no bulk translation and there never will be, because guardrail 1 means no model rewrites 214 entries into another language without 214 accepts. A prominent global language control that looks like a document toggle is therefore a promise the product deliberately does not make, and that is the real constraint on this decision, more than where the pixels go.

Options

A

Flags in the top bar

A permanent pair in the shell's top right, one tap to switch, visible from every screen.

Buys

  • One tap from anywhere, no menu to discover, and it is immediately legible to somebody who does not read the current interface language.
  • Bilingual users switching often, which is Lorenzo's own case, get the fastest possible control.

Costs

  • A flag is a country, not a language. Italian is also Swiss and Argentinian, and English forces a choice between two flags that has annoyed somebody in every product that ever made it. The app's own switcher already avoids this: its comment says endonyms are "the one label a language switcher must never need translating to be understood".
  • It sits in the top bar next to an entry whose own language is a separate, per-entry claim, so it reads as "translate this page", which is the one thing guardrail 1 forbids.
  • It spends permanent shell space on a control most accounts use once, and the top bar is where the palette, the quota meter and the account all want to be.
  • There is no top bar outside a universe today, so this option only exists if I3's option B ships first.
B

In the account menu, with Settings behind it

The shell footer carries the user. Clicking it opens the account menu, where Language is a row showing the current value as an endonym, and choosing it switches in place. The canonical control, with the sentence explaining what it does and does not do, stays on the settings page the menu also links to.

Buys

  • Solves I5 and I6 with one component, and gives the four unreachable settings pages and the privacy page an address in the interface.
  • Endonyms, so nobody has to argue about which flag stands for English.
  • Language sits among the other account preferences, which is what it is, rather than next to canon content, which it is not.

Costs

  • Two clicks instead of one for the bilingual user who switches daily.
  • Needs a real dropdown with focus management and an escape key, which is one of the three components I9 argues we should not hand-roll.
  • Depends on the shell existing outside a universe, or the menu is missing on exactly the pages where a new user is most lost.
C

A permanent pair in the sidebar footer

No menu. Two endonym-labelled buttons pinned under the nav, always visible, the way the current auth pages do it. The middle answer between a flag in the top bar and a row inside a menu.

Buys

  • One click, no new component, and LocaleSwitcher.svelte is reused rather than replaced.
  • Nothing depends on a dropdown, so it ships before I6 does.

Costs

  • Permanent sidebar weight for a rarely used control, in the footer where the account and the meter also belong. Three things in a strip that fits two.
  • Two-letter codes are worse than endonyms for the exact user this control serves, somebody who cannot read the current interface.
  • Leaves the other four settings pages exactly as unreachable as they are today.

I would take B, and the flags are the one part of the request I would push back on. The placement instinct is right, the control belongs in the shell and not on a settings page nobody can find, but the flag is the wrong glyph for a language and this codebase already argued itself out of it once, in LocaleSwitcher.svelte's own comment. If the top bar really is the right place, the honest version of A is the endonym as text, "Italiano" with a caret, which keeps the placement and drops the problem. The auth pages keep their compact switcher either way, because there is genuinely no account there yet, and they move it out of the top right per I2. It lands in the same shell issue I6's menu needs, since it is one row in that menu.

I6 · the account and the settings that have no home

Settings, and the account page that does not exist

Five leaf pages hold every account-level control the product has, and a sixth control, the account itself, has no page at all. apps/web/src/routes/settings/* is five unconnected screens, reachable only if you already know the URL, and none of them let you change the name or the email the product prints on every page you visit.

decidedB, the account menu plus one two-pane settings page, with a real Account pane holding name, email, password, sign out everywhere and delete. Its dependency on I3's shell is the schedule rather than a risk: I3 first, then this, and the menu is the same component I5 needs.

Today

  • No account page. The product prints the user's name and knows their email (apps/web/src/lib/components/shell/AuthStatus.svelte:21,38), and nowhere under apps/web/src/routes/settings/ can either be changed. There is no password change and no delete account.
  • Five islands, no index. routes/settings/keys/+page.svelte:49, export/+page.svelte:15, language/+page.svelte:27, billing/+page.svelte:26 and appearance/+page.svelte:24 each open with the identical "← Universes" link and nothing else in common; there is no routes/settings/+page.svelte to list them.
  • Two of five are reachable at all, and only from one place each. Home links only to appearance (routes/+page.svelte:59-64); the per-universe settings page links to appearance and export (routes/u/[universe]/settings/+page.svelte:26,29). Keys, billing and language have no inbound link anywhere in the app - not even from Sidebar.svelte:69-95, the one nav a signed-in GM actually uses.

Guardrail 5 the keys page is where the product says which provider sees your campaign content, and /privacy says the rest (F3, the contextual sentence on keys/+page.svelte:55-69 already links there). Options

Out of scope here on purpose: the per-universe settings page at /u/[universe]/settings, the writing switch and the precedence panel. A2 already gave that nav item to the universe; this decision is only about the account that sits above every universe.

A

A settings index

/settings lists the five leaves as rows, each with the sentence its own page already opens with. No shell, no menu, one new route.

Buys

  • Cheapest of the three: one route, no shell change, no new component.
  • Findable once, then bookmarkable - a GM who visits twice never has to search again.

Costs

  • Leaves the account gap open: still no name, email, password or delete control anywhere.
  • Still zero-click reachable from nowhere; a GM two levels into an entry still has to know /settings exists.
  • The guardrail 5 problem stays unsolved - keys is still a URL, not a move from anywhere.

Buys

  • The five islands stop being islands: one route, one sub-nav, matching the pattern the per-universe settings page already ships (issue #107).
  • Account exists for the first time - name, email, password and delete account get a home, closing the gap AuthStatus.svelte:38 has printed since issue #86.
  • Sign out and Keys are reachable from the footer on every screen the shell renders, which is what guardrail 5 needs.

Costs

  • Depends on the app shell existing outside a universe at all, which nothing ships today - Sidebar.svelte only mounts inside routes/u/[universe]/+layout.svelte. This option is the settings half of that fix, not the whole of it.
  • One more overlay pattern (the footer menu) that A3's palette does not route through yet.

Settings inside the shell's own sidebar

No menu, no separate page: choosing Settings in the account-level nav replaces the main pane with the sub-nav and its panes directly, the way the universe's own settings page already works today.

Buys

  • No popover state to design or build - one interaction pattern, reused from the universe settings page that already ships.
  • Settings is always visible as a row, not discovered by clicking an avatar first.

Costs

  • Puts a "Settings" row in an account-level nav next to the universe list; if that list ever grows past a couple of entries the row competes with A2's own seven-item cap for the same kind of space, even though it is a different list. Worth naming before it is built, not after.

  • The account identity (name) sits in a static footer with nothing to click; the product still has no one place that says "this is you" and lets you act on it.

I would take B. It is the only one of the three that gives the account a page, not just a route, and it collapses five orphaned leaves plus a bar that never leaves into one pattern the per-universe settings page has already proven (issue #107). It costs the most because it is the one that actually needs the app shell to wrap every route, not only universe routes - name that dependency up front rather than discover it mid-build. Once paid, it is what makes guardrail 5 true instead of aspirational: Keys and the link to /privacy are a footer click away from an entry, a proposal queue, or the home page, not a URL someone has to already know.

I8 · empty states

Ten sentences, one shape, and it is the wrong shape for most of them

Every empty list in Canonry renders the same way: one grey sentence, and nothing else. That is correct for some of these and wrong for most, because "nothing pending" and "no universes yet" are not the same kind of empty, and treating all ten identically means the one place a button belongs never gets one.

decidedA, one component, three variants. Cold start gets a primary action, a settled state stays a plain sentence and never grows a button that invents work, and a derived absence explains what it waits on. The copy stays in the i18n catalogue, not in the component.

Today

  • Home, cold start. "No universes yet - yours, or one you were added to, will show up here." (en.ts:956) - the one screen a brand-new account actually needs a button on has none.
  • Universe home, cold start. "Nothing here yet." (en.ts:949) - shown above.
  • Works, cold start, with a half-measure. "No works yet." (en.ts:886) - unlike the two above, this page does carry a create form (routes/u/[universe]/works/+page.svelte:47-53), but it sits below the fold as a full form, not as the one button a cold start wants next to the sentence that names it.
  • Proposals inbox, settled. "Nothing pending. Edit an entry to start a propagation run." (en.ts:386) - correct today: no button, no nag.
  • Review queue, settled. "Nothing left to review." (en.ts:434) - correct today, same reason.
  • Audit, settled. "No flags on this entry right now." (en.ts:277) - correct today, same reason.
  • Relations, derived. "No relations recorded yet." (en.ts:263) - names no dependency; a GM cannot tell whether relations run automatically or need an edit first.
  • Facts, derived. "No facts extracted yet." (en.ts:267) - same gap, and B4 already decided facts are on-demand, so the trigger exists and just is not named here.
  • History, derived. "No revisions yet." (en.ts:271) - same gap.
  • Images, the one accidental exception. "No images yet." (en.ts:288) already sits above a working Generate button (lib/components/media/EntryMediaPanel.svelte:223,242-250) - not because anyone decided cold starts need a primary action, but because F1's always-visible generate control happens to render there regardless. One surface got the right shape by accident; the other two cold starts did not get it at all.

Options

A

One component, three variants

An EmptyState component with three shapes: cold takes a sentence and one primary action, settled takes a sentence and nothing else, derived takes a sentence and an explanation that links to the thing it depends on. Every string still comes from the i18n catalogue - the component takes props, it owns no copy.

Buys

  • Works and home's cold starts finally get the primary button they are missing; relations, facts and history's derived absences finally say what they are waiting on.
  • One place to fix the shape everywhere at once rather than nine call sites drifting further apart, which is what already happened to works and images.
  • Copy stays in messages(locale) exactly like every other string in the product - the component takes sentence, an optional action: {'{'} label, href {'}'}, and an optional explain: {'{'} text, linkLabel, href {'}'}, never English literals.

Costs

  • Three call shapes to learn instead of one paragraph tag; a surface with a real fourth shape (there is not one in the inventory today) has nowhere to go without extending the component.
  • Someone has to decide, once, which of the three each of the ten existing empties is - the audit above does that work, but the next new surface has to do it again.
B

Per-surface, hand-written, kept deliberately

What ships today, kept on purpose rather than by default: no component, a checklist in the review rubric instead.

Buys

  • Zero component to design, build or maintain; each page keeps full control of its own layout.

Costs

  • A checklist is read at review time, not enforced at build time - it is exactly what let works' create form and images' accidental button drift into two different shapes already, with nobody deciding either on purpose.
  • Fixing the three real gaps (home, universe home, works' missing button; relations, facts, history's missing explanation) still means editing six files by hand today, and six more the next time a seventh empty state ships.
C

Illustrated empties

Drawn to reject it. A small line illustration above the sentence, warmer copy underneath.

Buys

  • Warmest tone of the three; might read as friendlier on a first-run empty universe.

Costs

  • No illustration budget and no icon set exist - H2 spent the product's only drawn asset on the mark itself, on the grounds that a raster gets redrawn from exactly one SVG. An empty-state illustration is a second art direction the product has not budgeted for and A1's reading room, paper and serif, was chosen precisely to not need one.
  • A decorative empty state ages the moment anything nearby changes; a doodle is harder to keep current than a sentence.

Guardrail 7 "won't stay that way" promises a populated, coherent future the product cannot certify. Canonry's whole stance is "here is what does not add up," never a promise that it will resolve itself - encouraging copy on an empty state is exactly where that promise starts leaking in.

I would take A. The audit above already sorts all ten existing empties into the three shapes with no leftover case, so the component costs nothing in new taxonomy, only in build. It fixes the two genuine cold-start gaps (home, universe home) and the one half-built one (works) with a real primary action, gives the three derived absences (relations, facts, history) the explanation they are missing, and leaves the three settled states exactly as quiet as they are today. It also folds images' accidental button into the same deliberate shape instead of leaving it as the one page that happens to be right. This locks in the contract for #16 relations, #17/#18 facts and history, #47 the proposal inbox, #55 audit and #20 works, so the next surface to ship an empty list, the players' wiki gap page (E7, #82-85) among them, reuses a decided shape instead of inventing a fourth kind.

I9 · the control layer

The controls nobody owns

This is not one screen, it is the reason every other screen in this pass reads slightly wrong without a reader being able to say why. apps/web/src/lib/components has fourteen feature folders and not one named Button, Input, Field, Badge, PageHeader or EmptyState, so every route hand-writes its own Tailwind for the same four controls. The tokens those controls should be built from are fine: routes/layout.css is a complete, careful system, both palettes, serif everywhere per G2, tabular figures, a real reading measure. This section counts how many different ways that system gets spent.

decidedC, shadcn-svelte as the control layer, with components of our own only where it has no answer. Taken against my recommendation of B, and the argument that wins is the one C's costs understated: the six components B would extract are the easy six, and the three that are actually hard, a dialog, a dropdown and a popover with real focus management, are the ones B still leaves to be hand-rolled. Four things this locks in, all verified against shadcn-svelte 1.5.0 rather than assumed. It expects its own token vocabulary, --background, --foreground, --primary, --muted, --border, --input, --ring, --radius, so the first task is one mapping block in layout.css from the reading room's names into that vocabulary, in one place, or every component gets restyled on arrival. Its dark variant is a .dark class, declared as @custom-variant dark (&:is(.dark *)), and this app themes on [data-theme='dark'], so that custom variant gets redefined against the attribute and G1's preference keeps working. The CLI copies component source into $lib/components/ui rather than adding a runtime dependency, which is what makes the restyle ours to do and ours to keep; what it does add is bits-ui, clsx, tailwind-merge, tailwind-variants and an icon set, all MIT and therefore fine to consume under AGPL. And the icon set is a design decision smuggled in as a dependency: this product has no icon language today, only the mark and a few glyphs, so either Lucide becomes that language deliberately or the components that reach for it get their icons replaced as they land.

Today

  • No component layer. The same "primary submit" role is spelled two ways with nothing between them: apps/web/src/routes/settings/language/+page.svelte:77 hovers hover:bg-accent-ink, apps/web/src/routes/onboarding/+page.svelte:55 hovers hover:opacity-90. apps/web/src/lib/components/ has folders for players, proposals, media, entry, audit, table, audio, onboarding, works, shell, auth, brand, ai and docs, and none of the fourteen is a shared control.
  • 73 distinct button-shaped class strings, not "roughly 40." I matched every class="..." on a <button> and every button-styled <a> (rounded, padded) across the app's 84 Svelte files: 90 <button> elements plus 11 button-styled links, 73 distinct strings, in 51 of the 84 files. Read them by role and the count does not fall: 18 read as primary (filled, bg-accent), 22 as secondary (bordered), 12 as plain text buttons, and 21 more are one-off shapes (pills, phone tabs, list rows) that fit none of the three. Padding alone splits the 18 primary strings six ways: apps/web/src/lib/components/table/QuickActionDock.svelte:56 is px-4 py-2.5, apps/web/src/routes/auth/sign-in/+page.svelte:120 is px-4 py-2.
  • Font size drifts on the same button. apps/web/src/routes/admin/models/+page.svelte:127's Save is text-xs; apps/web/src/routes/settings/appearance/+page.svelte:52's Save, doing the same job one click away in the product, is text-sm.
  • Inputs disagree on the same form field. Sign in's email field is border-line, px-3 py-2, at apps/web/src/routes/auth/sign-in/+page.svelte:102; the works form's title field is border-line-2, px-3 py-1.5, at apps/web/src/routes/u/[universe]/works/+page.svelte:60. 16 distinct input strings and 4 distinct textarea strings exist across 21 and 4 files respectively.
  • A hardcoded glyph nobody can press. apps/web/src/lib/components/shell/Sidebar.svelte:65 prints the literal string ⌘⇧A next to Ask the Loremaster, on every platform, for a shortcut with no listener: apps/web/src/lib/keys.ts:7-10 says outright "the shell only actively listens for isDismissKey today." Nineteen files away, apps/web/src/routes/u/[universe]/table/+page.svelte:317-318 calls formatShortcut() and renders Ctrl+K off macOS correctly. The cross-platform vocabulary A3/G3 asked for exists once, in keys.ts; one caller out of the app uses it.
  • Two disclosures, two implementations, both partial. apps/web/src/lib/components/shell/UniverseSwitcher.svelte:31-45 writes its own open state, its own outside-pointerdown listener and its own Escape handler for a menu. apps/web/src/lib/components/proposals/EvidencePopover.svelte:47-56 writes a second, unrelated one for a popover, with no outside-click and no Escape at all. Neither can be reused by the other, and the account menu I6 needs has nothing to start from.
  • The quota already has a home, just not the shell's. apps/web/src/routes/settings/billing/+page.svelte:59-79 already renders the real balance: included credits, purchased credits, warm budget remaining over warm budget total, tabular figures, correctly formatted per locale. It sits on a settings leaf nothing links to. F2 asked for a meter in the shell; the numbers landed with quota enforcement (#88) and the warm budget (#89), both closed, and the shell shows nothing.

Options

A

Leave it

Ad-hoc Tailwind everywhere stays the policy. A component wrapper is indirection over a language every contributor already reads, and Tailwind's own utility classes are, in this argument, already the design system.

Buys

  • Zero migration. No dependency, no wrapper API to design badly, no version to track against Tailwind 4 upgrades.
  • Every contributor already reads plain Tailwind; nothing new to learn to ship a screen.

Costs

  • The drift compounds. Every new screen adds a 74th spelling instead of reusing one of the 73 that already exist, because nothing forces a lookup.
  • G1's dark-mode review means checking 73 hand-written combinations against two palettes by eye, in 51 files, instead of one specimen sheet.
  • The mock above is the whole argument: a user feels this without being able to name it, and "the tokens are fine" stops being true in practice the moment nobody is made to spend them the same way twice.
B

Extract the six that earn it

Button, Field, Badge, PageHeader, EmptyState, Panel, chosen by call-site count rather than taste, with variants that match what the 73 strings already do rather than a new vocabulary invented on top of them.

Buys

  • One spelling of Save, one of Cancel, everywhere, chosen by counting call sites rather than by whoever wrote the newest screen.
  • This specimen sheet is the artifact a dark-mode review needs and does not have today: every variant, both palettes, one glance instead of 51 files.
  • EmptyState retires the copy-pasted "Nothing here yet." sentence (home, the entry list, the players' wiki gap page) into one component with a real call to action slot, instead of a sentence and nothing else.

Costs

  • 53 files are behind the new six and need a real migration pass, not a parallel option nobody adopts.
  • Call-site count is a blunt instrument: Panel is already just .card again, so the list is really five new components and one that already existed in every mock in this set.
  • Solves the styled-div problem and nothing else. The account menu, the universe switcher's disclosure and the evidence popover still need real focus management this does not provide.
C

Adopt shadcn-svelte properly

SPEC.md §11 already names shadcn-svelte in the apps/web stack; nothing in the app imports it. Draw what it buys where it is actually hard, not as a wholesale replacement for the six above.

Buys

  • A real dialog, dropdown and popover with focus trapping, arrow-key navigation and Escape-to-close, written once by people whose job is exactly that, instead of a third hand-rolled disclosure after UniverseSwitcher.svelte and EvidencePopover.svelte.
  • Finishes a decision already on record (SPEC.md §11) rather than opening a new one.

Costs

  • Its defaults are a different design language. Every component that lands gets restyled into the reading room before it ships; that is real work per component, not a free extraction.
  • Its own focus ring and accent are close enough to violet that a component landing unretokenised next to an AI surface risks Guardrail 2 before anyone notices, since violet is the copilot's colour and nothing else may spend it.
  • Adopting it for everything B already lists is a bigger lift than B is; most of B's list is a styled <div>, not a focus-management problem, and shadcn does not make a styled div cheaper to write.

I would take B, with C for the three parts that are genuinely hard to write well and are missing today: a dialog, a dropdown menu and a popover with focus management. Dropdown, for the account menu I6 needs from nothing and for UniverseSwitcher.svelte's hand-rolled disclosure. Popover, for EvidencePopover.svelte (C5), which today has a click toggle and no Escape. The fourth named spot, the image generation confirm (F1, G11), turns out to be the one place that already reaches for the platform: apps/web/src/lib/components/media/GenerateDialog.svelte:67-73 uses a native <dialog> and showModal(), so F1 already gets a focus trap and Escape for free without shadcn at all. That is the outlier, not the pattern, and it is worth keeping as proof the browser can carry the hard part when nobody has to reinvent it. Six components ship in days on call-site count; three real primitives, built once, close the account menu, the switcher and evidence review without a second bespoke disclosure ever getting written again.

Also decided, never built

Two shell parts are controls in the same sense as the six above: answered questions with no component behind them. The command palette (A3, G3) has its full cross-platform shortcut vocabulary already sitting in apps/web/src/lib/keys.ts, unread by anything except a test file and one static string, and it has no issue on the board: #75, which the register names against A3, closed having shipped table mode's instant search instead. The quota meter (F2) has its numbers already computed and rendered, just not in the shell. Drawn together, because they occupy the same sidebar and the same decision to finish rather than reopen.

Guardrail 7 the meter's copy states a real ceiling, "2,400 / 5,000," and never the word unlimited, even on a plan where the number is large. A meter that cannot be read as a promise is the only kind this product is allowed to ship.

I10 · the phone

A wiki you can only read at a table

Seventeen responsive utilities exist in the whole app, spread over eight files, six of them inside table mode. Everywhere else the shell assumes a desktop: the sidebar is a fixed 256px column with no breakpoint, so on a 390px phone it eats two thirds of the screen. E4 and G1 were both decided with a GM at a lit table in mind; prep on a sofa, or reading on a train, was never drawn.

decidedB, one responsive shell. E4's bottom tabs generalise past table mode, the sidebar collapses to a drawer, and the entry reader and the review queue are the two screens that have to survive at 390px. Priced as a pass over every route rather than a new screen.

Today

  • No breakpoint on the shell. The universe sidebar is w-64 flex-none with nothing conditional on it: apps/web/src/lib/components/shell/Sidebar.svelte:50.
  • Phone-aware, but only inside table mode. The one component with a real phone state is scoped md:hidden to that surface alone: apps/web/src/lib/components/table/PhoneTabBar.svelte:35.
  • Prep on a sofa was never drawn. Of the eight files carrying any md:/lg: class, six are table mode's own; the entry reader and everything else ship the same fixed shell as the desktop.

Options

A

Table mode only

Leave the phone as a table-mode surface, E4's four tabs, and let the rest of the app stay desktop. Cheapest option on the table: it costs a route audit, not a redesign.

Buys

  • Zero new surface area; E4 already shipped this and it works.
  • No risk to the one phone surface that's actually good today.

Costs

  • A GM cannot read their own wiki on the train, or check a name on the sofa before a session.
  • Leaves the Today squeeze exactly as broken as it is now for every non-table route.
B

One responsive shell

The sidebar collapses to a drawer behind the universe switcher; the top bar gains the palette and the account; E4's bottom tabs generalise from table mode to the whole app. Drawn at the two screens that have to survive: the entry, because it's the most visited, and the proposal queue, because it's the product.

Buys

  • The two screens that matter most, the entry and the queue, work one-handed on any device.
  • One tab shape, generalised from E4, instead of a second navigation pattern to learn.
  • The account and the palette, once it exists, both get a reachable home on phone too.

Costs

  • A pass over every route rather than a new screen: every surface's spacing, tap targets and overflow need checking, not just these two.
  • C6's keyboard queue (j k a r) doesn't reach a phone; the queue reverts to a per-card button pair there, a second interaction shape for the same surface.
  • The editor, import and table's authoring paths are not drawn here and still need their own answer.
C

Phone for reading, desktop for writing

Reading, Ask and table mode go to the phone. The editor, import and settings stay explicitly desktop: opening one on a phone gets a plain sentence, not a broken layout.

Buys

  • Never ships a broken editor: B2's toolbar and mention menu stay untested on touch entirely.
  • Honest about scope: a phone-shaped import review or settings panel was never asked for here.

Costs

  • A GM who drafts a quick note from a phone, mid-session, is told no rather than helped.
  • Still needs almost all of B's shell pass (drawer, top bar, tabs) to draw the reading half at all.

Rejected: swipe left or right to decide, on the queue. It reads fast on a phone, which is exactly the problem: guardrail 1 rules out anything that lets review collapse into one repeated gesture across many entries, and a swipe deck is that gesture wearing a nicer animation. B keeps one proposal focused with Accept and Reject as two separate taps, never a single motion that clears several at once.

I would take B, and price it honestly: it is a pass over every route's spacing and overflow, not a new screen, so it belongs to one shell pass rather than a ticket per surface. It also forces the one translation C6's desktop design never had to make: bare keys don't exist on a phone, so the review queue needs its button-per-card shape drawn for touch from day one, not bolted on after.

What I would do first

Ordered by what unblocks the most, not by how bad each one looks. The first three are a day and turn the preview from something that cannot be used into something that can.

  1. Fix the two defects. Drop the duplicated link on the home page, and put a prefers-color-scheme block behind the dark palette so Match system means something. Neither needs a decision.

  4. Extend the shell past the universe border. I3's option B. It is the structural fix, it is the largest single piece of work here, and every screen outside a universe stops being a floating column the day it lands.
  5. Then the two surfaces that are missing rather than wrong: the entry browser (I7) and the palette (A3, which has no issue and is the other half of I7).
  6. Then the pass work: the six components (I9), the empty-state pattern (I8), and the responsive pass (I10). None of them is a screen, all of them are the difference between this reading as a product and reading as a scaffold.

What I would not do. Redesign anything inside a universe. The entry reader, the proposal queue, the diff, the plan checklist, the import review and table mode are the product, they are built, and they are the parts that already look like the mocks. The problem is entirely in the ring around them.
