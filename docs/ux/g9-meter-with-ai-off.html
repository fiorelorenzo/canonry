Why this is a decision

C10 put the switch per universe and drew what it does to writing: Ask disappears, new proposals stop, whatever was already queued stays reviewable. F2 put a persistent meter in the shell, a chip beside the universe switcher, always visible, in credits and EUR. Neither page said what the meter shows for a universe that is off, and neither said whether "off" reaches semantic search, the embeddings behind it, and the retrieval that lets a derived universe read its base corpus (SPEC 4.1, 7). That last one is not a side detail: retrieval is also a model call, and it is also most of what makes Sword Coast (ours) more than a folder of 61 homebrew entries sitting next to a reference nobody can search.

Guardrail 4 the AI switches off completely, and what remains is a good wiki. Issue #107 states the bar without an exception clause: "no model call is made." C10 already used that same sentence once, to reject a warm cache left running quietly in the background; this decision is whether the same sentence also reaches search.

Options

A

Generation stops, retrieval keeps reading

Ask and propagation go dark. Semantic search, mention suggestions and a derived universe's live read of its base corpus keep running underneath, because they only read, they never write.

Buys

  • Nothing about the derived universe's value disappears: Sword Coast (ours) keeps reading Forgotten Realms exactly as before, so SPEC 4.1's whole reason for the derived kind survives the switch untouched.
  • Search stays as good as it always was, so a GM who mainly wanted the copilot's writing turned off, not its ability to help them find things, gets exactly that.
  • Cheapest of the three to build: retrieval's code path is untouched, only the generation and proposal endpoints get gated.

Costs

  • The meter keeps moving for a universe the GM was told is off, the exact silent-spend pattern C10 already rejected once for the warm cache, now reappearing in a different subsystem.
  • "No model call is made" (#107's own bar) stops being literally true the moment anyone types in the search box, so the guardrail's plainest possible sentence needs a footnote nobody wrote.
  • Auditing "is AI off for this universe" now means knowing off does not mean off for two whole subsystems, which is the opposite of guardrail 4's blunt promise.
B

Every model call stops, including retrieval

Off means off, literally: generation, proposals, embeddings, semantic search and a derived universe's base-corpus retrieval all stop together. Search falls back to plain keyword matching over Postgres.

Buys

  • "No model call is made" stays true without a footnote, in search, in a derived universe's retrieval, everywhere at once, the same bar C10 already set for background jobs, applied consistently instead of stopping at generation.
  • Auditing the switch is one sentence: check whether a model was called for this universe, not a list of exceptions someone has to remember and re-check by hand.
  • The graph, every accepted fact, and full keyword search stay completely intact, which is the actual content of "still a good wiki," not a claim about how the search box feels to use.

Costs

  • Sword Coast (ours) loses live access to the Forgotten Realms corpus the instant it is off; for a universe whose whole second half of content is that corpus, this is closer to losing the point of it than losing a convenience.
  • Search quality visibly drops: "the broken captain" no longer finds Aldric Vane's entry unless those exact words appear in it, which a GM will notice mid-session and may read as broken rather than as the switch working.
  • Turning off a homebrew universe like Valdoria Reach to save spend also turns off search quality nobody actually asked to lose; the two were never separable requests, but this option answers them as one.
C

A second, explicit toggle beneath the main switch

The universe-level switch still means everything off by default, as in B. A separate, off-by-default row lets a derived universe keep reading its base corpus on its own, named terms.

Buys

  • Lets a derived universe keep its whole reason for existing, reading Forgotten Realms, even with generation and proposals off, without doing it the silent way A does; the GM opts in explicitly, in writing, in Settings.
  • Matches C10's own ruling exactly: finer controls sit underneath the one clean switch, they never replace it, so full off still means everything off by default.
  • A homebrew universe like Valdoria Reach, with no base corpus to read, never shows the second row at all, so the common case stays exactly as simple as B.

Costs

  • Two switches per derived universe is two states an auditor has to check instead of one, re-opening the exact complexity C10 rejected its own per-feature option C for, just at a smaller scale.
  • The second row has to explain, in one line, the precise distinction this whole page exists to draw (generation versus retrieval) to a GM who may not have read this page.
  • Ships later than B: a second, clearly labelled, off-by-default toggle is a real feature to design and test, not a checkbox that falls out of the first switch's own implementation.

Rejected outright

Reroute retrieval to a free local embedding model when the switch is off, so it "costs nothing"

Tempting because the meter would stay honest without the search box degrading. It still fails Guardrail 4: the promise is that the AI switches off completely, not that it switches to a cheaper AI. Content still leaves the entry to be embedded by some model, which also reopens Guardrail 5, which provider sees campaign content and on what terms, for a subsystem the GM was told had stopped entirely.

What I would take

B, per universe, meter unchanged

Take B. Guardrail 4 reads as a blunt promise, not a scoped one, and C10 already drew the line for exactly this shape of question when it rejected a warm cache "left running quietly": a search index still being queried while the GM believes nothing is happening is the same trust break, aimed at a different subsystem. The honest cost, named plainly rather than glossed over: a derived universe with the Loremaster off cannot read its own base corpus, so Sword Coast (ours) becomes, for as long as the switch is off, exactly the 61 entries someone actually wrote, nothing borrowed live from Forgotten Realms. That is the real price of "still a good wiki" holding at all, not a detail to soften.

The meter: account-level only, exactly as F2 already drew it. F2's chip was never scoped to the open universe in the first place, its number is the whole account's spend, so there is nothing to hide or zero out when one universe's switch flips; a sibling universe may still be spending against the same included quota. Hiding the chip for an off universe would claim account-wide spend stopped when it did not, which is exactly the plain-words test Guardrail 5 sets. Showing a second, per-universe "zero" meter would invent a component nobody asked for and nobody can back with a number, since the plan's quota was never metered per universe to begin with. The one place the switch's state belongs is the universe's own settings row, exactly as C10 already drew it.

What the switch covers, concretely, so it can be built against:

Stops, no model call is made:

  • Ask, and the Ask page (C8, G5): no answers, no sources, the button does not render.
  • Propagation on save (SPEC 5.1): no new create, update or audit proposals to any entry.
  • Quick actions at the table that draft content (E3's "+ NPC here", "+ Inn here"); "Mark as revealed" and changing context stay, neither ever called a model.
  • Image generation: portraits, ambient packs (F1).
  • Warm pre-computation of any kind: briefs, NPC drafts, context packs, ambient warming (SPEC 8.1), no exception for background jobs, per C10's own precedent.
  • Semantic search and embeddings: the vector query behind "find," mention and relation-type suggestions while writing, ambiguous-match detection on import (D6).
  • Retrieval into a derived universe's pre-indexed base corpus (SPEC 4.1, 7): the Loremaster cannot answer from, or silently draw on, Forgotten Realms text; only the universe's own accepted canon is visible.
  • Import of new sources (SPEC 6): ingestion is itself a bounded model loop reading documents, not a parser, so a universe with the switch off cannot start a new import job at all.

Stays, no model needed:

  • Every entity, relation and fact already written or accepted; revision history keeps recording human versus ai_accepted (guardrail 2) exactly as C10 drew it, nothing is rewritten by the switch.
  • Plain keyword search over Postgres (title, alias, body text) in place of semantic search: slower to find the right sentence, but not gone.
  • Manual relation creation, a typed form in place of the one-click confirm, exactly as C10 drew it.
  • Manual entity creation, at the table and everywhere else.
  • Table mode's instant lane: pinned NPCs and relations, marking things revealed, playing back audio that was already generated.
  • The players' wiki (E7), export (F4), and everything already on the account.
  • Whatever already sits in the proposal inbox: nothing new arrives, nothing already there is discarded (C10, C6).

Cheapest way to find out this is wrong: ship B and watch two numbers. Support requests asking why a derived universe's Loremaster "forgot" Forgotten Realms would argue for C's explicit second toggle or gentler messaging about the loss. A spike in keyword-search complaints specifically on off universes would argue for a better non-semantic search (stemming, alias matching) rather than for quietly reintroducing embeddings through the back door.

What this locks in

  • #107 ("no model call is made") gets its complete, literal definition here: the two lists above are the acceptance criteria for that issue, not just generation and proposals.
  • #88's quota enforcement stays account-scoped: this decision explicitly rules out a per-universe variant of the ceiling or the meter being built alongside it.
  • #19's derived-universe retrieval gains its off switch here: "reads the official layer read-only" (SPEC 4.1) now has an explicit off state, not just an on one, and #19's own supersede behaviour is unaffected either way since it operates on the user's canon, not on retrieval.
  • Hard to undo: once a GM learns off means every model call, quietly narrowing that later (even to restore search quality) reopens the exact trust question C10 already closed once for the warm cache.
  • Deferred: option C's explicit second toggle, if a derived universe's GMs keep asking for search back without asking for writing back. Not decided here, and no id owns it yet.
