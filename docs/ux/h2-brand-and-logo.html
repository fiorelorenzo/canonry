Why this is a decision

Two properties now serve the public: the landing page on canonry.io and the product on app.canonry.io. Both are wearing the browser's default document icon and the word "Canonry" set in body text, which is what a mark decides: not whether we have a logo, but what the product looks like in a tab strip with forty other tabs, in a bookmark bar, and in the shell above every entry a GM writes. The palette and the type are already decided (A1 = B, the reading room; G2 = C, serif everywhere), so this is the one remaining piece of the visual identity, and it inherits every constraint those made.

Guardrail 7 the product says what does not add up, it never certifies that a canon is coherent. A mark built on a checkmark, a shield or a badge says "verified" before a word of copy is read, which is why one obvious option below is drawn and rejected rather than left off the page. C1 reserves violet for the copilot and forbids every other surface from spending it, so the mark cannot be violet: the identity is the reading room's burnt umber on paper. And the landing repository's own rule stands, no generated art in the marketing, so every option here is hand-drawn geometry that survives being rendered at 16 pixels, not an illustration.

Drawn and rejected

E

The accepted check

The obvious one, and the reason it is on this page is that somebody will suggest it later: the accept step is the product, so put a check in a frame.

rejected

Rejected on guardrail 7. A check is the universal glyph for "verified", and this product's central promise is the opposite: it says what does not add up and never certifies that a canon is coherent. A mark that claims verification in the tab strip contradicts the copy on the page it links to, and it is the one contradiction a careful reader would notice first. It is also what every AI tool in the category already uses.

What the choice locks in

Whichever mark wins, the same set of files ships and the same rules apply, so those are not part of the decision:

  • An SVG favicon as the primary icon, because it is the only format that follows the palette: one file, currentColor-aware, sharp at every size. A PNG pair (32 and 180 for iOS) and an ICO for the browsers that still ask for one, all generated from the same SVG rather than redrawn.
  • The mark alone in the app shell, where the universe switcher already carries the name, and the lockup with the wordmark on the landing page and in the Open Graph card, where nobody knows what the mark means yet.
  • Accent on paper, paper on ink, and the mark never spends violet, because C1 reserves it for the copilot and a logo that borrows it makes every marked proposal slightly less meaningful.
  • The wordmark is set in the product's own serif at its natural weight, not a display face nobody else in the interface uses. G2 decided serif everywhere with no exceptions, and a logo is not an exception.
