I7 · universe home

The entry browser that isn't

The sidebar's "Entries" item is the only door into a universe's canon, and it opens onto three sentences. With 214 entries behind that door there is no list, no type filter, no alias search, no sort and no pagination, and (verified by grep) no "New entry" control anywhere in the product either. docs/ux/index.html promised an entry browser and shipped a Recent list instead.

Today

  • No way to see the 214. The Recent list is the entire surface; there is no browser behind it, list, filter, alias search, sort or pagination, in apps/web/src/routes/u/[universe]/+page.svelte:33-57.
  • No "New entry" anywhere. Grepping the app for the string turns up exactly one hit, and it is a diff-card label for an AI-drafted entry, not a create action: apps/web/src/lib/i18n/en.ts:449.
  • The inventory promised this and it wasn't built. "Entry browser: list and filter 214 entries by type, alias, recency" has sat unbuilt against issues #15 and #104 since the audit: docs/ux/index.html:144.

Options

A

The browser is the home

/u/{slug} becomes the list itself: a type filter row, a search field that also matches aliases, and rows carrying name, type, an excerpt and when each entry last changed. Recent moves into the sidebar, where a version of it already lives.

Buys

  • Ships the inventory's own promise at the exact address a GM already clicks, no new nav item.
  • One list to build, not a dashboard plus a list, so it lands before the palette does.
  • Search-by-alias here is the only search this product has until A3 exists (issue #75).

Costs

  • Loses the one thing a dashboard would have shown: what changed, what's waiting, the quota.
  • A returning GM lands on a list, not a "here's where you left off" summary.
B

Overview, browser one click away

Home stays a dashboard: what changed, what's waiting for review, the quota, the current work. The browser gets its own address, /u/{slug}/entries. A2 caps the nav at seven and all seven are spent, so the "Entries" nav item repoints to the browser and the dashboard drops off the nav entirely, reachable only from the universe switcher, which A2 already exempts from the cap.

Buys

  • Keeps the "what's waiting, what changed" summary a returning GM actually wants first.
  • The quota meter (F2) and the current work get a real home instead of nowhere.

Costs

  • The dashboard is invisible in the nav; nothing tells a new GM it exists except trying the switcher.
  • Two surfaces to build and keep in sync instead of one.
C

One page, two modes

The home is the browser, exactly as in A, with a collapsible overview strip pinned above it carrying B's four signals: what changed, waiting for review, quota, current work. Collapsing it hands the full height back to the list; nothing needs a second address.

Buys

  • Both jobs at one address: no eighth nav item, no dashboard hidden behind the switcher.
  • The quota and review-queue signal, currently visible nowhere in the product, gets a home.

Costs

  • Denser on first load than A alone; the strip has to earn its 60px before a GM collapses it.
  • Collapse state needs to persist per user or every visit re-teaches the same click.
  • Still doesn't ship search itself, since A3/#75 owns the box; the field here has nothing to defer to until it exists.

Rejected: rank the list by an AI-guessed staleness score. 214 rows invite a "show me what's probably wrong first" sort, and that is a model's judgment steering what a GM reads before they've read anything. Guardrail 2 keeps violet, and everything it implies, for the copilot's own surfaces; a ranking signal baked into the browser's default sort would spend it on navigation instead.

I would take C. A gives up the one summary a returning GM wants and B hides the dashboard behind a switcher click nobody is told about; C buys both without touching A2's seven-item cap. It also keeps the honest caveat visible: the palette (A3, issue #75) is the other half of finding an entry and it isn't built either, so until it ships, this list's search field is the only search Canonry has, in any form.

I10 · the phone

A wiki you can only read at a table

Seventeen responsive utilities exist in the whole app, spread over eight files, six of them inside table mode. Everywhere else the shell assumes a desktop: the sidebar is a fixed 256px column with no breakpoint, so on a 390px phone it eats two thirds of the screen. E4 and G1 were both decided with a GM at a lit table in mind; prep on a sofa, or reading on a train, was never drawn.

Today

  • No breakpoint on the shell. The universe sidebar is w-64 flex-none with nothing conditional on it: apps/web/src/lib/components/shell/Sidebar.svelte:50.
  • Phone-aware, but only inside table mode. The one component with a real phone state is scoped md:hidden to that surface alone: apps/web/src/lib/components/table/PhoneTabBar.svelte:35.
  • Reading, not just table mode, has no phone treatment. Of the eight files carrying any md:/lg: class, six are table mode's own; the entry reader and everything else ship the same fixed shell as the desktop.

Options

A

Table mode only

Leave the phone as a table-mode surface, E4's four tabs, and let the rest of the app stay desktop. Cheapest option on the table: it costs a route audit, not a redesign.

Buys

  • Zero new surface area; E4 already shipped this and it works.
  • No risk to the one phone surface that's actually good today.

Costs

  • A GM cannot read their own wiki on the train, or check a name on the sofa before a session.
  • Leaves the Today squeeze exactly as broken as it is now for every non-table route.
B

One responsive shell

The sidebar collapses to a drawer behind the universe switcher; the top bar gains the palette and the account; E4's bottom tabs generalise from table mode to the whole app. Drawn at the two screens that have to survive: the entry, because it's the most visited, and the proposal queue, because it's the product.

Buys

  • The two screens that matter most, the entry and the queue, work one-handed on any device.
  • One tab shape, generalised from E4, instead of a second navigation pattern to learn.
  • The account and the palette (once #75 ships) both get a reachable home on phone too.

Costs

  • A pass over every route rather than a new screen: every surface's spacing, tap targets and overflow need checking, not just these two.
  • C6's keyboard queue (j k a r) doesn't reach a phone; the queue reverts to a per-card button pair there, a second interaction shape for the same surface.
  • The editor, import and table's authoring paths are not drawn here and still need their own answer.
C

Phone for reading, desktop for writing

Reading, Ask and table mode go to the phone. The editor, import and settings stay explicitly desktop: opening one on a phone gets a plain sentence, not a broken layout.

Buys

  • Never ships a broken editor: B2's toolbar and mention menu stay untested on touch entirely.
  • Honest about scope: a phone-shaped import review or settings panel was never asked for here.

Costs

  • A GM who drafts a quick note from a phone, mid-session, is told no rather than helped.
  • Still needs almost all of B's shell pass (drawer, top bar, tabs) to draw the reading half at all.

Rejected: swipe left or right to decide, on the queue. It reads fast on a phone, which is exactly the problem: guardrail 1 rules out anything that lets review collapse into one repeated gesture across many entries, and a swipe deck is that gesture wearing a nicer animation. B keeps one proposal focused with Accept and Reject as two separate taps, never a single motion that clears several at once.

I would take B, and price it honestly: it is a pass over every route's spacing and overflow, not a new screen, so it belongs to the shell issue #104 rather than a ticket of its own. It also forces the one translation C6's desktop design never had to make: bare keys don't exist on a phone, so the review queue needs its button-per-card shape drawn for touch from day one, not bolted on after.
