I1 · the door

The signed-out root, which is a landing page that was never built

Somebody who hears about Canonry and types the domain gets the application's empty home page: the wordmark, a broken sentence, and a link to appearance settings. F6 decided what belongs there over a year of arguing with itself, the demo as the hero with no copy above it, and G10 put the one sentence about export underneath it. Neither is built, so the product's front door is a screen written for a user who is already inside.

Today

  • The sentence is doubled. The link renders t.shell.signIn and the sentence after it is t.universe.list.signInPrompt, which already opens with the same two words, so the page reads "Sign in Sign in to see your universes." apps/web/src/routes/+page.svelte:23-26 against i18n/en.ts:955.
  • It never says what the product is. No sentence, no demo, no screenshot, nothing a stranger could act on. The one description that exists is the OG meta tag in the layout, which only Slack ever reads. routes/+layout.svelte:24-25.
  • Appearance settings is the only outbound link. Not docs, not privacy, not pricing. Guardrail 5 lives on /privacy and nothing in the interface points at it.
  • The mark is not a link and there is no header. Landing, home and error state are all the same route with three branches inside one <main>.

Options

A

An honest door, today, and the landing later

Keep / as one route, fix the sentence, and make the signed-out branch a door rather than an empty room: what this is in one line that survives guardrail 7, the two actions, and the links that have to be reachable. No demo, no marketing, one afternoon.

Buys

  • The domain stops looking abandoned, this week, without touching #3.
  • Docs and privacy become reachable, which guardrail 5 needs and nothing currently provides.
  • Uses copy that already exists and has already been through guardrail 7.

Costs

  • It is a text page. F6 chose the demo as the hero precisely because a sentence cannot show propagation, and this option does not show it.
  • Two things live at one address again, so #3 will rewrite this route rather than build beside it.
B

Build F6: the demo is the hero

The decided answer. The propagation loop runs above the fold with no copy over it: one entry, one edited sentence, the plan it produces, one diff with its evidence, and an accept that is the visitor's to press. Signing in is a quiet control in the corner. G10's lock-in sentence sits directly under the demo.

Buys

  • It shows the one thing no competitor does, in the twelve seconds a stranger gives it, and it shows it without a word of marketing.
  • The demo doubles as the honest version of the product: a proposal that waits, with its evidence attached.
  • G10 gets its sentence, in the one place a reader who cares about lock-in will look.

Costs

  • It is issues #3 and #4 in full, and the demo has to be a real interactive thing rather than a video, or it lies about latency.
  • Every string on it is guardrail 7 surface. "Canonry keeps your world consistent" is the sentence that will want to be written, and it may not be.
C

Two addresses: the landing is not the app

The app never renders a landing page. Signed out at / it redirects straight to sign-in, and the marketing site lives at its own address, which can then be a static build with its own cadence.

Buys

  • Marketing copy stops shipping on the app's release cadence, and the app stops carrying a route that has nothing to do with it.
  • The app's signed-out state becomes a single unambiguous thing: the sign-in page.

Costs

  • A second deployment target, a second TLS certificate and a second place for the brand to drift, for a product with one developer.
  • The two-stack preview and prod arrangement in SPEC 12 is per environment, not per site, so this changes the deploy story rather than extending it.

I would take B, and ship A this week as the interim. B is already decided and I am not reopening it; the only real question this surface asks is what the domain serves for the weeks or months before #3 lands, and today the answer is a broken sentence. A is two hours and uses copy that already passed guardrail 7. C is a real option and I would keep it in the drawer for the moment the landing page needs a pricing table, a changelog and a blog, which is not now.

I2 · auth

Sign in and sign up, the first screen anybody actually sees

Both pages are a left-aligned column stuck to the top of an empty page, with the language toggle as the second most prominent thing on the screen and the submit button smaller than the fields above it. Everything on them works. Nothing on them says a person designed them.

Today

  • The language toggle is the loudest control on the page. Two pills at the top right, at the same level as the wordmark, on a screen whose only job is a password. routes/auth/sign-in/+page.svelte:71. See I5 for where it belongs.
  • The submit is w-fit. Two full-width fields and then a small button, so the primary action is the least prominent thing in the form. routes/auth/sign-in/+page.svelte:117-123.
  • Nothing is centred and nothing is bounded. A 34rem column pinned to the top left of a 1440px page, which is why the screenshot has a third of a screen of empty paper under it.
  • There is no password recovery anywhere in the product. Not a link, not a route. A forgotten password is currently a database query.
  • The error is a paragraph. Rendered into the flow with no role="alert" and no aria-live, so a screen reader is never told the sign-in failed. routes/auth/sign-in/+page.svelte:125-127.
  • The mark goes nowhere. On the one page where a visitor may want to find out what this product is before typing an email into it, the wordmark is not a link.

Options

A

The same column, done properly

One card, centred in the viewport, mark above the title and linking home, full-width primary, recovery link in the form, language demoted to a quiet footer row with docs and privacy. Nothing structural, one file each.

Buys

  • Cheapest thing on this page that fixes the actual complaint.
  • Recovery, docs and privacy all get their link, and the error can get its role="alert" in the same edit.

Costs

  • A centred card is the most generic screen in software. It is correct and it says nothing about this product.
B

Split: the form, and the reason to fill it in

The card on the left, and on the right the product doing its one trick on the sample world: a sentence changed, a proposal drafted, marked as unaccepted and waiting. Static, no model, no network. Below 900px the right pane drops and this becomes A.

Buys

  • The one screen where a stranger decides whether to bother now carries the reason.
  • It is honest about the guarantee at the exact moment the guarantee matters, which is the argument guardrail 1 exists to win.

Costs

  • A second layout to maintain, and a right pane that is marketing copy inside the app, so it needs the same guardrail 7 review the landing page gets.
  • On sign-in, where the visitor is already sold, the pane is noise.
C

The title page

Full paper, the mark set large and centred, the form at 22rem under it and nothing else above the fold. The reading room applied to the one screen that currently looks like every other login form on the internet.

Buys

  • It looks like this product and not like a template, using only what H2 already decided.
  • One layout serves sign-in, sign-up, recovery and any future verification screen.
  • Nothing on it needs a guardrail 7 review, because it makes no claim.

Costs

  • Makes no argument, so it leans on the landing page having done that work. Somebody who arrives directly at sign-up from a friend's link learns nothing.
  • Vertical centring on a short laptop viewport needs care or the footer rule pushes the form up awkwardly.

Guardrail 2 Rejected outright: an auth screen painted in the copilot's violet. It is the most tempting thing on the palette and the one colour these pages may not spend. C1 reserves violet for unaccepted AI text, and a product whose sign-in page is violet teaches every user that violet means Canonry rather than "a model wrote this and you have not accepted it". The mark itself follows the same rule and stays burnt umber, per H2.

I would take C for sign-in and B for sign-up. The door is quiet and the decision to sign up gets the argument, which is the same split F6 already made between the demo and the app. They share one layout: B is C with a right pane, so this is one component with a slot, not two designs. Whichever wins, the four defects in the flaw list are fixed in the same commit, since the recovery link and the role="alert" are not design questions. Blocks #86.
