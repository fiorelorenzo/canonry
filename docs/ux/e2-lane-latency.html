Why this is a decision

§8's three lanes are declared budgets, not a UI. A spinner is the one thing they rule out on principle: if nothing at the table may wait on a model, then a control that visibly waits is advertising a rule the product just broke. What is not fixed is what a card looks like before it is warm, and §8.1 adds a second wrinkle: warm material is pre-computed on a schedule the GM cannot see, so a "fresh" card can go stale while nobody is looking, and staleness is not an alarm; it regenerates at the next trigger, not now. If this gets decided by accident, the first component built against it likely reaches for a loading state, which is the exact pattern the rule exists to prevent.

Guardrail 2 AI text is visually distinct until accepted. A warm NPC draft is unaccepted AI text by construction, it is a proposal waiting on the GM, so whatever marks "still warming" cannot be the same visual language as "already yours."

Options

A

Progressive arrival, quiet marker

Content streams in as it becomes ready. Anything not yet ready carries a small pulsing dot, nothing that asks for attention.

Buys

  • No layout shift once a card exists: it fills in place, it does not appear from nothing.
  • Reads as "alive" rather than "loading", which matches nothing actually being blocked.
  • One visual vocabulary, a dot and a caption, covers all three lanes without new chrome per lane.

Costs

  • A GM glancing quickly can miss the dot and read the dash as simply nothing there.
  • Cold and warming look identical except for dot colour, easy to confuse at a dim table.
  • Streaming several cards in over a few seconds is more moving parts than a static screen.
B

Explicit freshness indicator

Every card, warm or not, always carries a visible freshness line: fresh, warming with an estimate, or stale and why.

Buys

  • Says out loud that stale is normal and does not mean "regenerate now", which is exactly what §8.1 needs the GM to internalise.
  • A GM deciding whether to trust a card for a dramatic reveal has the information to decide.
  • Showing the old brief while the new one warms means something is always on screen, never a gap.

Costs

  • A badge on every card, all the time, is chrome the GM did not ask for on 90% of glances.
  • Estimates ("~6s") are themselves a small promise the warm queue may not keep under load.
  • Busiest option on a phone screen (E4), where every pixel of a card is already spoken for.
C

Nothing visible

A card exists once it is ready. Until then there is no card, no marker, no reserved space. The affordance simply is not there yet.

Buys

  • The plainest possible reading of "nothing waits": there is nothing to wait for, because there is nothing there.
  • Zero chrome spent on a state (warming, cold) the GM cannot act on anyway.
  • Cheapest to build: a card either renders from data or it does not render.

Costs

  • Indistinguishable from a bug: is Sennah not pinned, not warmed yet, or did the query fail?
  • A GM cannot tell "will appear in 4 seconds" from "will never appear this session" without asking.
  • Nothing invites the on-demand slow-lane fallback (Iselde's portrait) because there is no card to hang the "generate now" button on.

When the warm budget runs low, §8.1's fixed degradation order decides what stops being pre-computed first, not a fourth lane, but what happens before a lane even starts.

Rejected outright

Swap the draft into the entry the moment it finishes warming

The tempting version of "progressive arrival": once a pre-generated NPC brief or portrait is ready, just show it as the entry's content instead of surfacing it as something to accept. It reads as a nice disappearing act: no queue, no click, and it is exactly what pre-computation is not allowed to do. Guardrail 1 propose, never apply: a warm artifact is a proposal until a human accepts it, regardless of who paid to generate it early. Guardrail 2 compounds it: once it is silently "just there", it stops being visually distinct AI text, and revision history can no longer say whether a human or an accepted proposal wrote it.

What I would take

A, with C's honesty about what will never warm

Take A. Progressive arrival with a quiet marker is the only option that scales down to a phone screen (E4) without turning into a wall of badges, and it does not ask the GM to learn what "stale, regenerating, ~6s" means mid-scene. The one thing I would take from C: a card that will never warm this session (Iselde's portrait, once the warm budget is spent) should say so plainly rather than sit forever with a pulsing dot that never resolves. A dot that lies by omission is worse than no dot.

Cheapest way to find out this is wrong: put option A in front of someone mid-session and time how long before they ask "is it stuck?" about a warming card. If that happens inside a scene, the quiet marker is too quiet and B's explicit label is worth the chrome after all.

What this locks in

  • #73's instant-lane cards render immediately with this same warm/warming/cold vocabulary even though they never wait on anything. The pinned NPC card and the pre-warmed brief share one component, not two.
  • #77's five warm triggers need a client-visible state machine (warm, warming, never-warmed) that this decision names; the trigger logic and the card state have to agree on what "stale" means.
  • #79's ordered event stream is what makes A possible at all: cards must arrive and update in the order they were requested, or "progressive" reads as "random".
  • Locks the degradation order (media first, then drafts, then text) into the UI as a real fact the GM can be told ("no portrait this session"), not an internal detail.
  • Deferred: the accept flow for a finished draft once it does arrive belongs to C2 and C6, not here.
