Why this is a decision

SPEC §5.2 fixes the mechanics hard: audit runs on the sub-graph a recent edit touched, produces at most a handful of flags, and each one carries the two statements that disagree. What is open is where a flag surfaces: and just as much, in exactly what words, because Guardrail 7 can be broken by a technically correct layout wearing the wrong copy. "3 issues found" reads as a verdict no matter which corner of the screen it sits in. The number worth holding in view while writing any of it: LLM contradiction detection sits around F1 ≈ 52%, barely better than a coin flip at telling a real contradiction from two true statements that merely sound like they disagree. A flag is a question addressed to the GM, never a finding addressed at the canon.

Guardrail 7 never promise consistency. The product says "here is what does not add up"; it never says "your canon is consistent," and copy implying the second is a bug.

Options

A

A section of the proposal surface

An "Audit" section in the aside, next to Proposals.

Buys

  • Same place the GM already checks after an edit: no separate surface to discover.
  • Room to show both statements side by side without truncation.
  • "At most a handful" reads as a short list here, not as an interruption.

Costs

  • If the GM never opens that aside this session, the flag sits unread the whole time.
  • Sitting next to Proposals invites the same posture as an editable diff, and a flag is not something to accept or reject.
  • Needs its own visual weight to avoid reading as a fourth kind of proposal at a glance.
B

A badge on the entry

A quiet marker on the entry itself, where the disagreement actually lives.

Buys

  • Surfaces exactly where the contradiction lives: open Aldric Vane, see there is something about Aldric Vane.
  • A quiet badge, not a queue item, is honest about what a 52% F1 detector can promise: a look, not a task.
  • Costs nothing to repeat: both Aldric Vane and Cairnmouth carry the same badge, pointing at one flag.

Costs

  • Invisible until the GM happens to open one of the two entries involved.
  • No single place to see everything a recent edit's audit produced.
  • A badge that only shows a count risks reading as a health indicator over time, "0" looks like a pass, which guardrail 7 forbids implying.
C

A periodic digest

Flags collect into a weekly or session-boundary list.

Buys

  • Batches naturally with a GM's own rhythm instead of interrupting mid-edit.
  • One list, one place to write the disclaimer once, instead of restating it per flag.
  • Cheap to build on top of A or B, as a second read of the same data.

Costs

  • A contradiction about tonight's scene sitting unseen for up to a week defeats the point of catching it near the edit.
  • "At most a handful" already caps volume; batching solves a noise problem that does not exist here.
  • A digest that arrives whether or not there is anything worth reading habituates the GM to ignore it.

Rejected outright

An aggregate consistency score on the universe

"Valdoria Reach: 94% consistent" is the kind of number a dashboard wants to show, and it is exactly what Guardrail 7 forbids by name: SPEC §3 singles this phrasing out directly. With contradiction detection near F1 52%, a percentage across the whole graph is closer to noise than a metric, and it implies a certainty the two-statement flag underneath it was never built to support.

What I would take

B as the surface, reading the same list A would show

Take B: the badge lives where the disagreement actually is, and it is a pointer into one underlying flag list, not a second copy of the data: the same aside section from A exists regardless, as the one place to see everything a recent audit produced, reachable without having opened either entry. They are not competing surfaces. C is worth reconsidering only if audit volume per universe grows past "a handful," which the cap makes unlikely by design.

Cheapest way to find out this is wrong: count how often a GM opens the Audit section without having first seen a badge on an entry. If that number is not near zero, the badge is not earning its place and the aside list alone would have been enough.

What this locks in

  • #55 ships with both the badge and the aside list reading the same underlying flags, and with the wording from the table above verbatim, not a rewritten version of it.
  • Once "worth checking, not necessarily wrong" ships as the framing, tightening the language later toward certainty (even something as small as "check this") walks back a promise the whole product depends on. Any future copy change here should be checked against guardrail 7 before it ships.
  • Deferred: a digest or notification surface (option C) is not covered by any current decision id. If audit volume ever grows past a handful, it needs its own decision, not a retrofit of this one.
