Why this is a decision

G9 decided the behaviour: with a universe's Loremaster off, generation and proposals stop, but semantic search, mention suggestions and a derived universe's live read of its base corpus keep running, because reading is most of what makes this a good wiki. That behaviour is not the problem; the words next to it are. Guardrail 4 promises "the AI switches off completely, and what remains is a good wiki," and a GM reading "AI off" on a universe whose content is still leaving for an embedding provider on every keystroke has been told something the product does not do. Guardrail 5 requires that be stated in plain words, not discovered from a bill. And F2's shell meter (2,400 / 5,000 credits included, 180 / 600 warm budget) already moves for reading, so a GM who flips a switch labelled "off" and then watches that number keep climbing has hit the exact silent-spend pattern SPEC 15 built the warm budget's own visible line to prevent. Two questions, one switch: what is it called, and does reading spend the quota.

Guardrail 4 "the AI switches off completely, and what remains is a good wiki. Someone who turns everything off must still get their money's worth." Guardrail 5 "which provider sees campaign content, what retention, no training on customer data, stated in plain words." Every option below has to pick a label and a spend rule that both survive being read literally by a GM who has never seen this page.

Options

A

Rename the switch to what it does

"AI off" becomes "Stop writing." One switch, same scope C10 already gave it, honest about what keeps going.

Buys

  • The word on the switch and the behaviour underneath finally agree, which is what guardrail 4 actually asks for: it never required reading to stop, only that the label not claim it did.
  • No new setting, no new mental model: C10's one switch per universe stays exactly where it is.
  • The meter needs one sentence, not a new section, because the number was already true; it just needed an owner.

Costs

  • A GM who wants a universe to spend nothing, ever, has no control that does that; "stop writing" cannot be read as "stop everything" no matter how it's worded.
  • The rename has to propagate everywhere C10's old copy appeared, including the toggle button text and any onboarding copy that already says "AI off."
  • Still requires guardrail 5's plain words about the embedding provider to live somewhere near the switch, not just on F3.
B

Keep "AI off," make reading free

The name stays. Retrieval and embeddings for a switched-off universe stop drawing on the included quota, absorbed as the cost of the wiki still working.

Buys

  • "AI off" survives unchanged: no copy to hunt down and rewrite across onboarding, tooltips or the toggle itself.
  • Directly answers the meter-honesty half of the problem: a GM watching the number after flipping the switch sees it stop, which is what "off" implied in the first place.
  • Gives a genuinely free tier of "off" to a cautious GM who wants to try switching a universe off without any residual bill.

Costs

  • Retrieval still calls an embedding provider and still leaves the account's own infrastructure cost unchanged; "free" describes the quota line, not the actual spend, which is exactly the kind of gap guardrail 5 exists to prevent if left unstated.
  • Creates a per-universe carve-out in how the shared meter counts, which every other reading of that meter (F2's whole design) now has to special-case.
  • Invites a workaround: switch every universe off to get free retrieval, then switch back on only to write, which is a heavier interaction for a saving nobody asked to optimise for.
C

Two lines in Settings

A writing switch and a reading switch, reading defaulting to on. "Everything off" becomes reachable for the GM who wants it, without changing the honest default for everyone else.

Buys

  • The default a fresh GM sees is exactly the behaviour G9 decided, named plainly, with no promise the product does not keep.
  • A GM who genuinely wants zero model calls for a universe, for privacy or cost reasons, has a real control that does that, satisfying guardrail 4's literal reading for whoever needs it.
  • Sword Coast (ours), a derived universe whose entire value is reading Forgotten Realms, can turn writing off while reading keeps working, without a single misleading label anywhere.

Costs

  • Two switches to explain instead of one; the sentence under them has to teach the distinction the first time a GM sees it.
  • A GM who only reads the first row and stops there may still believe "off" covers everything, which is the same failure this whole page exists to prevent, just moved one row down.
  • More states to test against "no model call is made": writing on/reading on, writing off/reading on, writing off/reading off, and the one nobody should be able to reach, writing on with reading off.

Rejected outright

"AI off" left unchanged, with reading spending the quota unlabelled

Doing nothing, the state this page exists to close, means a switch that says AI off while Valdoria Reach's search and mention suggestions keep calling an embedding provider and keep moving the Included quota number underneath it. That is not a design option, it is the bug Guardrail 4 and Guardrail 5 were written to catch: a plain word promising more than the product does, and a spend that is not stated at all, let alone in plain words. Whatever else ships, this exact combination, this label with this silence, cannot.

What I would take

A, rename the switch to "Stop writing"

Take A. It fixes the actual defect, a label that overclaims, with the smallest change: one string and one sentence, not a new setting or a new exception in how the meter counts. B keeps the old label and tries to make the sentence carry an exception few GMs will read closely enough to trust, and it complicates F2's meter with a per-universe carve-out for a saving that was never the point of the switch. C is the more complete answer, a real path to literally zero model calls, but it answers a need nobody has surfaced yet: G9 was decided precisely because full-stop retrieval defeats the product's own reason for existing on a derived universe, and giving every GM a second switch to reach that state trades one sentence of honesty for a second control most will never need and some will misread as the first one already covering.

Cheapest way to find out this is wrong: watch support conversations that start "I turned AI off but you're still charging me" after the rename ships. If that phrase keeps showing up despite the new label and sentence, naming alone isn't clearing the confusion, and it argues for adding C's reading switch underneath A's writing switch, not for replacing A with B.

What this locks in

  • #107 keeps its per-universe scope from C10, and its bar, "no model call is made," now applies only to the writing half of the switch; the issue's acceptance criteria need that split written down explicitly so a test against "no model call" isn't written against reading by mistake.
  • #88, the quota meter's behaviour with the AI off, gets its answer here: the meter keeps counting reading for a switched-off universe under Option A, and needs the one added line naming which universe that spend belongs to, or the silent-spend pattern SPEC 15 built the warm budget line to prevent reappears one level up.
  • #109, guardrail 5's plain-words requirement, is satisfied by the sentence under the switch, not by F3 alone: F3 covers the contextual disclosure at the moment content leaves for a provider in general, this page covers the specific moment a GM has just been told something is "off."
  • Deferred: a second, reading-side switch (option C) is not covered by any current decision id. If support traffic shows GMs need a literal zero-spend state, it needs its own decision, not a retrofit of this one.
