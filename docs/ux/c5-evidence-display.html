Why this is a decision

The spec is explicit about the shape of evidence (which entry, which sentence) and explicit about what it is not: a number. That leaves the actual reading experience open, and it leaves a harder problem unaddressed: what does the UI say when the honest evidence is weak? A relation with a hop count is confident by construction. A candidate reached only by embedding similarity has nothing that firm to point to, and dressing that up as equally solid evidence is worse than showing nothing.

Guardrail 3 every proposal shows its evidence: which entry, which sentence, never an arbitrary confidence score. This page decides where that evidence sits on screen, and what stands in for a confidence number when the system wants to say "this one is a stretch."

Options

Two proposals, drawn under each treatment: The Valdoria Watch, reached by a direct relation (strong evidence), and Cairnmouth, reached only by embedding similarity to "the thaw after the Sable Winter", no relation to Aldric Vane, no textual mention of him, a fishing town two days up the coast that the Sable Winter also touched. It is the weakest evidence in the fixture set, which is exactly why it is the test case: whatever a treatment does with Cairnmouth is what it actually does under guardrail 3, not what it does in the easy case.

A

Source sentence, quoted above the diff

A persistent quote block sits above every diff: the exact source sentence, plus the retrieval reason in plain words. Always visible, no interaction.

Buys

  • Evidence is read before the diff, in the order the model itself worked: source, then proposal.
  • On screen the moment the diff is, which matters most exactly when the channel is weak, as with Cairnmouth.
  • One place for both a quote and a plain-word reason; no separate component to build.

Costs

  • Ten diffs means ten quote blocks stacked above them, and most quote the same Aldric Vane sentence.
  • Takes vertical space from the diff itself, the thing actually being decided on.
  • For strong relation-sourced candidates the quote often repeats what the relation label already says.
B

Popover on the changed text

The changed span carries a dotted underline (.ev); hover or click opens the evidence in a floating box (.pop). Costs nothing until asked for.

Buys

  • Costs nothing until wanted; a GM who trusts the diff reviews ten entries without reading ten quotes.
  • .ev/.pop already exists in the shared kit with click support wired for keyboard and touch.
  • Scales to any number of proposals without stacking real estate.

Costs

  • The one case guardrail 3 exists for (a stretch reached only by embedding similarity) is exactly the one an unopened popover hides.
  • Hover does not exist on the table-mode surfaces (E4); click-only has to be the real behaviour everywhere, not a fallback.
  • A GM skimming ten accepts can miss that Cairnmouth's evidence was worth a second look; nothing on the closed state signals that.
C

Permanent evidence column

A docked column runs beside the diff, one card per proposal, always visible, never opened or closed.

Buys

  • Every proposal's strength is visible at a glance, weak ones included, without opening anything.
  • Reading order is the reader's choice: content first or evidence first, unlike A which fixes the order.
  • Room for a longer, specific reason on the embedding case, not competing for space inside a 320px popover.

Costs

  • A three-column layout (nav, entry, evidence) is tight before B1 has settled the sidebar's own width.
  • The Debts of Valdoria scene candidate lives in a work node, not an entry; a docked column assumes parallel real estate a work view may not have.
  • Ten evidence cards visible at once for a ten-entry plan is the review screen shouting evidence at a GM who asked about one diff.

Rejected outright

A bare confidence number, "87% match"

The most familiar pattern from every recommender system the team has used elsewhere, and the literal thing Guardrail 3 forbids: "never an arbitrary confidence score." It is also the wrong instrument for Cairnmouth specifically, an embedding cosine similarity dressed up as a percentage implies a precision the model does not have, and it is exactly what makes a stretch look as trustworthy as a direct relation.

Skip evidence for "obvious" relation-sourced candidates

Tempting as a decluttering move: a one-hop commands relation feels self-evident, why quote it. It still breaks Guardrail 3, which says every proposal, not every uncertain one. A relation can itself be wrong or stale; that is the entire premise of the Iselde Wrenn candidate, whose relation is the thing being corrected, so the source stays visible even when confidence feels high.

What I would take

B, with A forced open for embedding-only proposals

Take B as the default: costless for the nine cases out of ten that do not need a second look, and it already exists as a component. But B's own cost list names the exact failure this decision cannot ship with: the weakest evidence is the easiest to skip. So the rule is not "B everywhere," it is "B, except." Any proposal whose only channel is embedding similarity, with no relation and no textual mention, renders with its evidence forced open, A's treatment, not hidden behind a click. Cairnmouth never gets the option to be skipped past; the strong, ordinary cases keep B's quiet default.

The stand-in for a confidence number, in every treatment above: the retrieval reason, in plain words, always present: relation commands, 1 hop for a strong case, similar wording only, no graph link for a weak one. It is honest in both directions without ever being a score.

Cheapest way to find out this is wrong: once the proposal model is instrumented (#47's outcome field), split accept rate by retrieval channel against whether the evidence was ever opened. If embedding-only proposals get accepted without their forced-open evidence ever being read, forcing it open did not work and the channel needs a harder gate than visibility, maybe a confirmation step instead.

What this locks in

  • #47 the retrieval reason and the source span are fields on the proposal row, not text generated at render time, so both the quote and the plain-word reason survive a page reload and are queryable for the accept rate split above.
  • #51 evidence lives on the same per-entry diff screen the accept and reject buttons live on; it is never a separate step the GM can accept past.
  • Hard to undo: once "forced open for embedding-only" ships, quietly relaxing it later to reduce clutter is the same mistake guardrail 3 exists to prevent, just introduced after the fact instead of at launch.
  • Deferred on purpose: whether rejecting a forced-open, embedding-only proposal needs a different one-word reason set than a normal reject. That belongs to C7.
