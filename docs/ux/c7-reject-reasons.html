Why this is a decision

SPEC §5.1 is specific about what a reject is for: "rejection asks for a one-word reason, which is training signal for the ranking, not a survey." That sentence fixes the intent and rules out one whole family of answers, but it does not say what the word looks like on screen, and the gap between a labelled signal and a survey is exactly one extra field wide. Get it wrong toward richness and the ranker in §5.1's candidate step gets years of free text it cannot use as a feature; get it wrong toward silence and it has nothing at all, and the ~10-entry cap stays exactly as noisy as the day it shipped.

Guardrail 1 reject is the other half of the same per-entry action accept is. Whatever this asks for has to fit inside the click that already exists in C6's queue, not open a second screen the GM has to close before moving on.

Options

A

Chips with a free text escape

Five fixed words, one click, with an "Other" field for the rest.

Buys

  • As fast as the reject it follows: one more click, not a form.
  • Five fixed words are a feature the ranker can use directly, unlike free text.
  • "Other" catches what the five miss without forcing everyone through a text box.

Costs

  • Five words will not fit every reason a GM has; some rejects land on "Other" anyway.
  • The chip set becomes a small taxonomy someone has to maintain as the product grows.
  • A GM in a hurry may pick the nearest chip rather than the true reason.
B

Free text with suggestions

A single input, ghost suggestions underneath, nothing pre-selected.

Buys

  • Captures nuance a five-word set cannot, in the GM's own language.
  • Suggestions still nudge toward a shared vocabulary without enforcing it.
  • Feels considerate: the product is asking, not filing a category.

Costs

  • Typing anything is slower than tapping a chip, right where speed matters most (C6's rhythm).
  • Free text is not a ranking feature until something else classifies it, which is one more model call SPEC does not budget for.
  • An open box invites exactly the survey SPEC §5.1 rules out: sentences, not signals.
C

Nothing at all, inferred from behaviour

The reject just happens; the signal comes from what the GM does next.

Buys

  • Zero friction: rejecting stays a single click, forever.
  • No vocabulary to design, translate, or maintain.
  • Cannot be gamed by a GM clicking the nearest chip to move on.

Costs

  • A proxy signal is not a reason; "edited within 2 minutes" also matches a GM fixing something unrelated.
  • Nothing to show the GM their own reject history means, which is its own small transparency gap.
  • Every proxy has to be invented and tuned before it exists, unlike a chip label that is just text.

Rejected outright

A required reason form: category, free text and a severity picker

Tempting because it is the most information, and the most familiar shape (every bug tracker has one. SPEC §5.1 rules it out by name: the reason is "training signal for the ranking, not a survey." A required multi-field form also breaks Guardrail 1's working rhythm: reject is supposed to be as light as accept, and a form that blocks progress until three fields are filled makes rejecting slower than accepting, which quietly teaches GMs to accept things they are unsure about just to keep moving.

What I would take

A, five chips plus an escape hatch

Take A. It keeps reject exactly as fast as the accept it mirrors, which matters because C6's queue depends on that symmetry: pressing r should cost about as much attention as pressing a. It also gives the ranker a small labelled vocabulary it can actually use as a feature, instead of B's free text (which needs a second model to classify before it means anything) or C's proxies (which are correlations wearing a reason's clothes). The fixture reasons: wrong, already true, not canon yet, too much, prose, already cover the range a GM would give in practice; that is the starting five, not a placeholder set.

Cheapest way to find out this is wrong: track how often "Other" gets picked against the five fixed chips. If free text dominates, the five words do not cover what GMs actually mean, and the fix is renaming or expanding the chip set: a copy change, not a rebuild of the mechanism.

What this locks in

  • #56 ships against this exact enum: wrong, already true, not canon yet, too much, prose, plus an open "other" bucket the ranker can ignore. The categories are the contract, not just the field.
  • What the signal is used for: "wrong" and "already true" are strong negative weights on the candidate-finding step itself (the cheap model in §5.1); "too much" tunes the ~10-entry cap's relevance cutoff; "not canon yet" behaves closer to a snooze than a penalty, since the fact might resurface once the world catches up; "prose" is not a ranking signal at all: it routes to whichever step writes the diff text, not the one that finds candidates.
  • What it must never do: never block progress waiting for a reason (skipping is a valid outcome: the reject already happened before any chip is chosen); never re-ask about a proposal already rejected; never surface an aggregate "why GMs reject" nag inside the working flow itself. That view, if it exists, belongs to F5's metrics surface, not this screen.
  • Renaming or merging chips after real rejections are logged against them loses historical comparability for the ranker: treat the five as fixed once #56 ships, extend rather than rewrite.
  • Deferred: an aggregated view of reject reasons for the GM or for the team is F5's decision, not this one.
