Why this is a decision

An entity in SPEC 4.2 is not a page of prose with a title. It carries mandatory aliases that mention detection depends on, typed relations that render an inverse label on the other side, atomic facts each holding the exact span of text they came from, images, what the players have discovered of it, and which scenes of a work use it. None of that is decoration; it is what the propagation loop reads. Get the anatomy wrong and either the graph data has nowhere honest to live, or it takes over the page and the entry stops being something a person would want to read or write.

The trap is specific: SPEC 4.2 says typing is inferred and confirmed with one click, never imposed as a form to fill in. An entry page that opens on an empty grid of required fields for a brand-new character has already lost, before a single word of prose exists.

Guardrail 2 AI text stays visually distinct until accepted, and revisions keep recording whether a change was human or an accepted proposal afterwards. Whatever holds the structured layer (type, relations, facts) has to be able to show that distinction, not just the prose.

Options

A

Document with everything in the margin

The prose is the whole page. A narrow margin column carries short notes ( a relation, a fact, an alias, loosely aligned to the paragraph that supports them, like footnotes that never ask you to look away for long.

Buys

  • The prose stays the undisputed centre of the page, which is most of what a GM does here.
  • A relation reads next to the sentence that justifies it, which is a genuinely nice proximity.
  • No new mode to switch into; the margin is always there, always current.

Costs

  • Facts, images and history do not stay proportional to prose length; a thin entry has empty margin, a rich one overflows it.
  • Images cannot live in a 190px column at any useful size.
  • Alignment to "the right paragraph" gets fragile the moment prose is edited and things reflow.
B

Record with a fields spine above the prose

A compact bar under the title: type, aliases and the key relations as chips, all in one glance, then the same prose document underneath, undecorated.

Buys

  • Fastest possible scan of what an entry is: type, names, connections, in one bar.
  • A dense list of entries (proposal review, search results) can reuse the spine as a preview row.
  • Clear separation between "structure" and "writing" for anyone who thinks in database terms.

Costs

  • The chips repeat what the prose already says, so the two can drift if one is edited and not the other.
  • Reads closer to Kanka's field-first page than SPEC 4.2 wants, even without a single empty box.
  • Still has nowhere for facts, images or history; it solves relations and stops there.
C

Document plus a switching right column

The document stays exactly what it is today. The right column, already home to Proposals in A1, gains tabs: Relations, Facts, Images, History.

Buys

  • Room for everything SPEC 4.2 and 4.4 actually attach to an entity, without crowding the prose.
  • Reuses the aside real estate A1 already spent, so it is a tab bar added, not a new column invented.
  • Facts keep their span attached and hidden until asked for, matching how evidence is meant to surface.

Costs

  • A one-click switch to see relations is still a click; option A shows the top ones without any.
  • Four tabs is one more thing to notice exists, for a GM who never scrolls past the prose.
  • The aside disappears under 900px, so this entire layer needs a fallback on narrow screens.

Rejected outright

Auto-applying the inferred type and first relation on save

Skipping the one-click confirm and just writing the inferred type and the first obvious relation straight into the entity the moment the entry is saved would make a new entry look instantly complete, chips and all. It is exactly what Guardrail 2 forbids: an AI-inferred fact rendered indistinguishable from something the GM actually confirmed, with no visual marking and no accept step behind it. SPEC 4.2's "never imposed as a form" is not a licence to go the other way and impose it silently instead.

What I would take

C, because the document should not have to change shape as data grows

Take C. It is the only option that scales with an entry rather than against it: Aldric today has three relations and three facts and no portrait, but a fully written faction leader might have a dozen of each, and neither A nor B holds that gracefully. A's margin either overflows or goes mostly empty depending on the entry; B's spine solves relations and nothing else, and duplicates data the prose already states. C keeps the document exactly as readable as it is today and gives the structured layer room to be exactly as big as it needs to be, tab by tab.

Cheapest way to find out this is wrong: build the Facts tab first against an entry that actually has ten or more extracted facts, not the three shown here, and see whether a flat list still reads or whether it needs grouping by paragraph. If it needs grouping, that is a change inside the tab, not a reason to abandon the tabbed column.

What this locks in

  • #15 the aliases shown in the kicker and the type shown in the badge are not copy; they are the exact columns this issue ships, so the entry header has to read and write them live, not display a cached label.
  • #105 the editor B2 designs has to open somewhere on this exact page, next to this exact spine or margin, without duplicating the relations or facts this page already shows in the aside.
  • Deferred on purpose: how a relation is actually created and confirmed with one click belongs to B3. How much of the extracted fact layer is shown by default, versus behind a toggle, belongs to B4. What the "used in Scene 3" panel looks like belongs to B5.
