Why this is a decision

A1 chose the reading room, option B: warm paper, serif prose, one of the three skins on the table and the only one of the three that shipped no dark theme at all. That is a fine answer for prep, an hour at a desk reading and writing. It leaves table mode stranded, because E1 already decided table mode is a mode the whole app switches into, and E4 already put that mode on a phone at a lit table, one hand on the screen and the other on dice. Both pages were drawn against a dark screen before A1 was settled, on the assumption that A1's own recommendation would ship: a dark palette as a first-class theme, with table mode defaulting to it. Lorenzo took the reading room instead. E1's note claiming A1 had settled the dark default has been corrected to point here, and every table mock in E1 to E5 now renders on the warm dark candidate below rather than on the mint console palette of the option nobody took. What none of that answers is the actual question: whether table mode goes dark at all, whether it is a whole-app preference, or whether it stays on the same paper and just gets louder.

Guardrail 2 AI text has to stay visually distinct until accepted, and tracked after. C1's dashed underline and violet margin marker, and C4's diff colours, were tuned once, against one palette. A repaint is exactly the kind of change that can leave a marking technically present and quietly unreadable, worth checking before either dark option below ships, not after.

Options

Whichever of these ships, two surfaces are out of scope and stay light regardless. The players wiki (E7) shows undiscovered content as a gap page, which only reads as an honest blank on the same paper everything else is written on. The landing page (F6) is the public demo, shown to people who have never set a preference of any kind. Neither is redrawn below.

A

Table mode carries its own dark skin

The .dark wrapper is not a preference, it is what "table mode: on" already means. E1's toggle re-skins the whole app for as long as the mode is on; when it is off, table mode has never existed and nothing is dark.

Buys

  • Smallest possible surface to audit dark: only what E1, E3 and E4 already draw needs a second pass, not diffs, queues, or admin tables.
  • One rule, no setting: "table mode" and "dark" are one word, not two things that can disagree.
  • Matches what E1 and E4 already assumed and drew; shipping this costs nothing those pages did not already spend.

Costs

  • A GM who just prefers dark for ordinary prep, glare, headache, taste, has no way to get it outside a table session.
  • If a GM taps through to Aldric Vane's full entry mid-session, which E1's own figcaption says they can, that entry, and everything in it including C1 and C4, is now dark too, whether or not that pairing was ever tested.
  • Ties two things together that might not always travel together: "I am at the table" and "I want dark" are usually the same GM, but not provably always.
B

Dark as a whole-app preference

A setting, not a side effect: dark can be turned on for the entire product, prep included, and table mode simply defaults to whatever the preference already is when it turns on.

Buys

  • Serves the GM who just wants dark, at a desk, at 2am, with no table anywhere near them.
  • One preference, one place to set it, rather than a rule bundled invisibly into a different toggle.
  • A GM can be at the table in daylight and stay light, or prep in the evening and go dark, independently.

Costs

  • Every screen the reading room was designed for, diffs, queues, D3's buckets, F5's admin tables, needs a dark pass on day one, not only the four E1–E4 surfaces.
  • Two things can now disagree, table mode on, preference off, which E1's decided mock never anticipated and would have to be redrawn to show.
  • A second full palette to keep in sync with every future component, forever, instead of one bounded extension.
C

No dark at all, table mode turns the paper up

Table mode never repaints. It raises contrast and size on the exact same warm paper tokens, bigger type, heavier ink, thicker borders, and stops there.

Buys

  • Removes the repaint risk entirely: C1's marker and C4's diff colours are exactly the ones already tuned for this palette, nothing to re-verify.
  • One palette, forever, for every surface the product has or will add; G2's serif boundary never has to be re-litigated per theme.
  • A screen at full brightness is still a smaller worst case at a dim table than a half-finished dark theme with a stray bright popover in it.

Costs

  • Gives up the one advantage dark genuinely has at a table: less light thrown at the other players' faces, less glare on eyes already dark-adapted from the room.
  • E1 and E4 were both drawn and argued against a dark screen; this option quietly reopens both, since "taller rows are also the phone layout" was never argued as "also the light phone layout."
  • A GM who has used a hundred other dark-by-default apps at their table gets one more product that does not match the room, for no reason visible on the screen itself.

The two places a repaint breaks, side by side, in the shared .dark tokens: C1's decided marking and C4's decided in-place diff. Both already carry a dark-mode override in ux.css for the colour that matters most, ins/del backgrounds and the primary button's text, but not for everything the marking touches.

Look at the "1" inside the marker on the left. It is white text on --ai, a deep violet on paper (#6b4ee6) and a pale one in the dark tokens (#b8a2ff). White on pale violet is close to unreadable at 9px. The diff on the right is fine as drawn: ins.i and del.d both already carry a .dark override in ux.css, .mrk does not.

A third thing broke building this page, worth recording since it is bigger than either marking: body { color: var(--ink); } resolves once, against whichever --ink is in scope at <body>, always the light one, and every element that never re-declares its own color inherits that frozen value straight through a nested .dark wrapper. Both mocks above had to add an explicit color: var(--ink) to their own bare text (.dh2 b, .pin h5, .entry h1.title) to read at all in dark; before that fix, "The Valdoria Watch" and the pin names were rendered in full but invisible, dark ink on dark paper. The same gap is already live: E1's own decided mock (the four pin cards above) and C1's dark tab both show this exact failure right now, entry and character titles rendered and unreadable, not hypothetically but on this server today. Whichever option ships here, fixing this belongs in ux.css itself, one rule on .dark/[data-theme="dark"] re-asserting color, not a per-component patch repeated on every page that touches dark.

Rejected outright

Fix the marker's dark contrast by recolouring it for that theme

The cheapest patch for the "1" above is to give the marker a different hue on dark, table mode's own accent, gold, reads fine at 9px against dark, rather than --ai. It is exactly wrong. Guardrail 2 spends one hue, and only one hue, on "this is a proposal, not yours yet." A marker that is violet in the reading room and gold at the table means a GM relearns the cue every time they cross the threshold, and worse, gold at the table is already the accent on every ordinary button and mention chip there, exactly the "decoration, not warning" failure A1 already rejected violet-as-brand-accent for. The fix is a darker ink on the marker's white numeral, not a different hue.

What I would take

A, table mode carries its own dark skin

Take A. It costs the least to actually finish: E1, E3 and E4 already reasoned and drew against a dark table screen, so scoping dark to exactly the surfaces those pages cover, and nothing else, ships a palette that has already been stress-tested rather than opening a second one. B is the more generous answer for a GM who simply likes dark, but it prices every present and future surface into day one, D3's buckets, F5's admin tables, the palette itself, before any of them has been checked against it, which is a second reading room to maintain rather than a bounded extension of the first. C is the safest for the marking bug above, since it never triggers it, but it throws away the one real advantage a dim room gets from a dark screen, and reopens two already-decided pages that were drawn assuming otherwise.

Cheapest way to find out this is wrong: ship the marker fix above, then put E4's phone screen, dark, in front of a GM who is not at a table, at a desk, in normal light, and see whether they turn table mode on anyway just to get the dark skin. If they do, "table" and "dark" were never the same preference, and B was the right answer, which is a setting to add, not a redesign to undo.

What this locks in

  • #104 the shell owns the toggle either way, but under A it only has to flip a class for the duration of a mode, not persist and restore a standing preference across sessions and devices.
  • #72 E1's context-declaration widget ships against the real .dark/[data-theme="dark"] tokens now in ux.css, not the bespoke mint palette its own mock still hardcodes; that mock is stale the moment this page records a decision.
  • #81 E4's bottom-tab phone screen is confirmed dark by construction, and the marker contrast fix above has to land before it ships, since a pin card there could carry the same unaccepted-text marking an entry does.
  • Hard to undo: once a GM learns table mode always means dark, decoupling the two later, adding a dark preference that stays off during a session, breaks a habit formed at the table, not just a stylesheet.
  • Deferred on purpose: whether a dark preference for prep ever ships at all is not closed by taking A here, only deprioritised behind the smaller, already-drawn surface.
