Why this is a decision

SPEC 4.2 is precise about the data: relation is one row between two entities, and the side you are reading from renders whichever label: forward or inverse belongs to that direction, straight from relation_type. That much is fixed. What is open is entirely about the interaction: where does a GM confirm that a mention has become a typed relation, and how much of the catalogue behind it (label, inverse label, cardinality, the entity types each end allows). Do they ever have to look at.

Get this wrong and the product regresses to the two tools it is explicitly not trying to be. A dropdown-and-autocomplete form on every mention is Kanka. A GM who edits each side of a relationship separately, from each entity's own page, is World Anvil, and is also how the two sides drift: nothing stops "Aldric employs Corvin" existing next to "Corvin does not employ Aldric" once they are two independent rows instead of one.

How that looks a month later is B4's decision, not this one. And Guardrail 3 means the click is next to the sentence that justified it, never next to a bare confidence number.

Options

A

In the margin

Relations live in the entry's aside, always visible, the way A1's exemplar already draws them. Confirmed rows sit at the top; a stale one carries a one-click retype; a freshly inferred one arrives as its own small card with the evidence sentence and nothing else. No relation_type dropdown lives here at all.

Buys

  • Matches A1's exemplar exactly, so it costs nothing to build twice.
  • Relations stay visible while reading, which is when a GM notices one is wrong.
  • Prose is never interrupted; the mention keeps reading like a sentence.

Costs

  • The margin is contested: proposals, relations, and (per B4) provenance all want it.
  • A relation from a paragraph the GM has scrolled past is easy to miss.
  • No room for the catalogue if a GM genuinely wants to browse it.
B

In a dialog

The aside stays a plain list with a "1 pending" line. Opening it is the only way to act on an inferred relation, but the dialog has room for the full catalogue behind a disclosure, for the GM who wants to see what else character → faction allows.

Buys

  • Full catalogue on demand, without ever defaulting to a form.
  • One focused decision at a time; nothing competes with it on screen.
  • Aside stays short: a count, not a growing stack of inference cards.

Costs

  • A modal is a stop; it is exactly the "form to fill in" feel SPEC 4.2 warns against, even with one button in it.
  • Reviewing several pending relations means opening and closing several dialogs.
  • The evidence sentence is quoted, not the live paragraph, so context is a step removed.
C

Inline, in the sentence

The mention that produced the candidate gets a small dot; directly under the paragraph, a one-line strip carries the inferred label, the confirm button, and a short, cardinality-filtered alternative. The aside relations list becomes read-only reference.

Buys

  • Zero distance between the evidence and the decision: they are the same line.
  • Nothing to navigate to and back from; the click is where the eye already is.
  • A paragraph with three mentions can carry three independent, small decisions.

Costs

  • A dense paragraph of prose grows a stack of strips, which fights B1's document read.
  • Only room for a short filtered list, never the catalogue. A GM who wants to browse it has nowhere to go.
  • Once confirmed the strip disappears and the interaction leaves no trace at the mention, so a second read cannot tell what was decided there.

Rejected outright

An "add relation" button on each entity's own page

It is the natural instinct: Aldric's page gets a button that writes "employs → Ashen Ledger", the Ledger's page gets its own button that writes "employed by → Aldric". SPEC 4.2 is explicit that this is wrong: relation is "one row, never two, so the two sides cannot drift apart." Two independent writes can end in one accepted and one rejected, one edited and one forgotten: a contradiction inside the graph itself, which is exactly the class of thing Guardrail 7's audit, running at F1 ≈ 52%, is unlikely to ever catch. This is the World Anvil and Kanka failure the assignment names, and it is a schema violation, not a taste call.

A relation_type form on every mention

Type dropdown, cardinality shown as a hint, an autocomplete for the far end, a submit button. It is the most "correct" data-entry pattern and SPEC 4.2 forbids it by name: "typing must be inferred and confirmed with one click, never imposed as a form to fill in." A form on every mention is what makes Kanka feel like data entry instead of writing, and it punishes the GM for every sentence that happens to name two things at once.

What I would take

A, with B's catalogue disclosure as its escape hatch

I would take A. It costs nothing against A1's exemplar, which already draws relations in the aside, and it keeps the one thing prose needs most: nothing appearing mid-sentence while the GM is trying to read it. The inference and cardinality filter get the type right often enough that a full catalogue browser is a rare need, not a daily one.

For that rare case, "not this" on the inferred card does not open a new UI. It expands the same small disclosure table B draws in its dialog, right inside the aside card. That way A and B are not really competing designs, they are the common case and the escape hatch for the uncommon one.

Cheapest way to find out this is wrong: ship it against a homebrew world with unusual relation_types (titles, oaths, debts, the kind of thing Valdoria Reach has plenty of) and watch how often "not this" gets clicked. A low rate means the aside was enough. A high rate on esoteric catalogues means the catalogue needed to be reachable on its own, not just as an escape hatch, and that is a much smaller change than switching to B outright.

What this locks in

  • #16 the relation_type catalogue has to be queryable well enough to power ranking (which type is inferred first) and the cardinality-filtered alternates, not just a lookup table for rendering the inverse label.
  • #15 aliases[] gets a second write path: the one-click confirm here, separate from editing the field directly in an entry's own form.
  • A confirm here still writes a revision with author_kind = ai_accepted, exactly like a full propagation accept. B4 owns what that looks like afterwards, and it is a real question: a relation confirm is a much smaller event than a ten-entry propagation accept, but the record it leaves has to be findable the same way.
  • Deferred to C2: whether a not-yet-confirmed inferred relation is also a proposal row that shows up in the general proposal surface, or a lighter mechanism scoped to mentions only. Both are defensible; this page assumes the latter but does not decide it.
  • Hard to undo once shipped: GMs learn where relation editing lives within a session or two. Moving it later (aside to dialog, say) is a retraining cost, not just a redesign.
