Why this is a decision

Everything text-shaped goes through Cloudflare AI Gateway; images go through the same gateway to Replicate; a GM's own machine through Spole is a stated future, not fiction. None of that is secret, but none of it is written down anywhere a GM reads before they paste their campaign into a text box either. Guardrail 5 does not just require the facts to be true, it requires them stated in plain words, and "plain words" is a placement decision as much as a copywriting one: a fact that is true but three clicks deep is not transparency, it is documentation.

Guardrail 5 which provider sees campaign content, what retention, no training on customer data; stated in plain words.

Options

A

A public trust page

A standalone page, linked from the footer and from account settings, that a visitor can read before ever signing up.

Buys

  • Answers the question before signup, for a skeptical visitor doing due diligence.
  • One page to keep accurate as providers or terms change.
  • Linkable from support, from the landing page, from anywhere.

Costs

  • A page that exists is not the same as a fact stated in plain words at the moment it matters; most GMs will never click a footer link.
  • Says nothing about BYO key, which is an account-level control, not a policy statement.
  • Duplicate content risk with F6's landing page, which needs its own short version of the same facts.
B

A settings panel

"Data & providers" in account settings, with the BYO key fields directly underneath the facts they change.

Buys

  • The right neighbour for BYO key: a control and the fact it depends on live together.
  • Findable by anyone who thinks to check settings, and durable once found.
  • Natural home for detail: retention windows, per-provider terms, links to their policies.

Costs

  • Requires an account to see at all, so it cannot reassure anyone before signup.
  • Settings pages are where people go to change something, not to read a policy; it can sit unread indefinitely.
  • No prompt at the moment content actually leaves, which is when the question "who sees this" is most likely to occur to someone.
C

Contextual, at the moment content leaves

A short, exact sentence next to the action that sends something out, every time that action is the kind that spends.

Buys

  • Plain words exactly where guardrail 5 means them: at the moment the choice is being made, not before or after.
  • Cannot go stale unread; it renders every time the action that makes it true is taken.
  • A natural place to surface "use my own key" as an inline offer, not a buried setting.

Costs

  • Repeated across several surfaces (import, generate, Ask), each of which has to carry an accurate, current sentence; more places for the copy to drift.
  • Too terse on its own for retention detail or a full provider list; needs a link out to somewhere fuller.
  • A GM who wants the whole picture before doing anything has nowhere dedicated to read it first.

Rejected outright

No disclosure beyond a link to the Terms of Service

This is the industry default and exactly what Guardrail 5 exists to rule out: the facts might be true and still buried in legal text nobody reads before pasting a campaign into an import. "Stated in plain words" is not satisfied by a document that states things in legal words.

BYO key asked for during signup, or required to raise quota

SPEC 15 is explicit: BYO-key stays available, never the default. Asking for a provider key before a GM has seen any value contradicts the product's own "minutes to first value" promise (SPEC 2) and reframes an optional convenience as a toll. It also implies the included plan is somehow lesser, which is not true; it is the same routing either way.

What I would take

C as the moment it is said, B as where the detail and the key live

Take C for the sentence itself: the fact has to be true exactly when a GM is about to send something, and nowhere else reaches that moment reliably. Every one-liner links to B, the settings panel, for the full provider list, retention wording and the actual key fields; so the contextual note stays short without becoming incomplete. A does not need to exist as a fourth surface: the one paragraph a pre-signup visitor needs belongs on the landing page itself (F6), not on a page built and maintained solely for this decision.

BYO key, spelled out: turning it on stops that provider's calls from drawing against the included quota or the warm budget, and your own provider's rate limits apply instead of ours. It does not change model routing; the cheap-model-for-candidates, premium-for-diffs split (SPEC 5.1) still happens on your key exactly as on ours; and it does not route around the gateway, so cost accounting and caching stay uniform (SPEC 11.1). Generated images still carry the same "Generated" marking and the same guardrail 6 privacy regardless of whose key paid for them.

Cheapest way to find out this is wrong: ship the contextual sentence first, without the settings panel finished, and see whether anyone clicks "Full policy" at all. If nobody does, the settings panel can shrink to just the key fields and the prose moves back into the tooltip.

What this locks in

  • #90 the disclosure sentence ships as real, reviewed copy attached to every spend-triggering action, not a generic "AI is used here" notice written once.
  • #109 the BYO key toggle lives in the settings panel this decision defines, off by default, with the quota and routing behaviour above as its literal spec.
  • Hard to undo later: once the per-action sentence names specific providers (Anthropic, OpenAI, Replicate), changing providers means updating copy across every surface it appears on, not one settings page.
  • Deferred: the landing page's own short version of this (F6) is written there, not duplicated here word for word.
